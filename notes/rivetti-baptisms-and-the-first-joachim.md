## 1. THE FIRST JOACHIM FALCO — baptised 25 November 1774, dead at three

**Ark `3Q9M-CSD3-13H2-1`**, Arienzo baptisms, the 1774 leaf.

> «Anno D[omi]ni Millesimo Septingentesimo **Septuagesimo quarto 1774**, die vero **vigesimaquinta 25
> Novembris**, R[everendu]s D. **Fortunatus Canonicus Bruno** Substitutus baptizavit infantem natum
> **ex MATTHEO FALCO, et FRANCISCA CRISCI** conjugibus hujus Parochiae, qui [nominatus est …]»

And in the margin, beside a drawn cross:

> **✝ JOACHIM FALCO — obiit die 11 Decembris 1777**

**Matteo Falco and Francesca Crisci had a son called Gioacchino who died at three years old.** The
Gioacchino this archive follows — the *colono* of strada Camellara who died on 14 February 1851 — was
baptised **2 February 1779**, fourteen months after the first one was buried. **He carries his dead
brother's name.**

This is the second time this family has been caught doing it. **Giovanna Falco** died on 15 September
1805 and a second Giovanna was given the same name afterwards. The pattern is ordinary for the period
and is exactly the trap that makes a name alone useless as an identifier here.

**The founding couple's documented children rise to SIX**: Angela, Rosa, Pasquale, Giuseppe,
Gioacchino — and now **the first Gioacchino, 1774–1777**, who never reached the civil registers at all
because he died thirty-two years before they began.

> **What it does NOT do.** It does not rescue the «about 1775» this archive derived from the 1851
> death act's stated age of seventy-six. 1774 is tantalisingly close to that — but the marginal
> *obiit* says this child died in 1777, so he cannot be the man who died in 1851. **The correction
> published this morning stands: Gioacchino was seventy-two, and the clerk guessed.**

## 2. CHIARA RIVETTI WAS BAPTISED 14 DECEMBER 1766 — and was 77, not 80

**Ark `3Q9M-CSD3-136K-R`**, the same parish, the 1766 leaf. Margin: **«Clara Rivetta»**.

> «Anno D[omi]ni Millesimo Septingentesimo **Sexagesimo Sexto**, die vero **decimaquarta 14 Decembris**,
> Rev: D. **Fo[rtunatus Canonicus Bruno]** baptizavit infantem natam **ex NICOLAO RIVETTA et ANNA
> DI NUZZO** conjugibus hujus Archipresbyteratus della Collegiata di S. Andrea Apostolo Terrae
> Argentii, cui imposui nomen **CLARA**. Matrina fuit **Cecilia Martone**.»

Her civil death act of **13 September 1843** gives her age as **eighty**. The baptism makes her
**seventy-seven**. One more stated age that was wrong; the baptism wins.

### AND A CONFLICT, PUBLISHED AS A CONFLICT

| | father | mother |
|---|---|---|
| **Parish baptism, 1766** (read from the image) | Nicolao Rivetta | **ANNA DI NUZZO** |
| **Civil death act, 1843** | Nicola Rivetti, *contadino* | **ANNA DI RUGGIO** |
| **Civil death act of her brother Carmine, 1821** | Nicola Rivetti, *contadino* | **ANNA DI RUGGIO** |

**Both readings are certain in their own document.** «di Nuzzo» is unmistakable in the hand, and the
parish repeats it across five children. «di Ruggio» is written twice in the civil series, twenty-two
years apart. This archive does not know which is the woman's own name and does not guess. The most
likely explanation is that one of the two is what somebody *heard* — but that is a guess, and it is
labelled one.

### The two Nicola Rivetta — both real, and the 1843 rule-out confirmed from the other side

The archive once had to choose between two couples who fitted Chiara's Latin burial entry: the gentry
**Don Nicola Rivetti × Donn'Anna Quotolo**, and the labouring **Nicola Rivetti × Anna**. The 1843 act
wrote the father's trade — *contadino* — and ruled the gentry couple out.

**The parish books now show both households existing side by side**, distinguished by the clerk himself:

- «Mag[nifi]co **Don Physico D. NICOLAO RIVETTA**, e Mag[nific]a **ANNA RUOTOLO**» — the **physician** —
  children baptised 28 Dec 1768 (Francisca Hippolita), 1772 (Maria Vittoria Teresia Elisabetta),
  and Anna Maria Angela Joanna. Every one of them given three or four names, as gentry children were.
- plain **NICOLAO RIVETTA** and **ANNA DI NUZZO** — children given one name each.

Chiara is in the second house. **The rule-out was right, and is now right from two directions.**

### THE GIOVANNA PROBLEM, STATED AND NOT SOLVED

This archive already holds **a Giovanna Falco, daughter of Pasquale Falco and Chiara Rivetti, who died
15 September 1805**, and **a second Giovanna born in 1812** whom it published as a daughter given her
dead sister's name. **This baptism is a third, in 1810.**

Three possibilities, none of them tested:

1. the 1810 baptism and the 1812 civil birth are **the same child**, and one of the two dates is wrong;
2. they are **two different children**, and the 1810 girl also died young;
3. **more than one Pasquale Falco × Clara** is at work, as has already proved true of Giuseppe Falco.

**It is left open.** Naming it is worth more than resolving it wrongly — and the rule this archive
added this week applies exactly here: *check a find against what is already held in the same pass
that publishes it.*
