# Falco — key findings

Established 7 September 2026, from the MyHeritage tree, FamilySearch full-text, and published local history.

## THE BREAKTHROUGH: Pasquale Falco's baptism, and the real generation 1

**Arienzo baptisms, 24 October 1767** (`3Q9M-CSD3-1362-V`):

> Anno Domini millesimo septingentesimo **sexagesimo septimo**, die vero vigesima quarta 24 …
> D. Clemens Crisci de nostri licentia baptizavi infantem natum ex **Mattheo Falco**, ed
> **Francisca Crisci** coniugibus huius archipresbyteralis collegiatae ecclesiae Sanctae Andreae
> Apostoli **Terrae Argenti**, qui nominatus est **Paschalis**. Matrina fuit Rosa Villano.

This names Pasquale's parents and **replaces Agostino Falcone**, who was never there. The head of the
documented line is now **Matteo Falco & Francesca Crisci**.

Note the birth year: the tree said 1765; the register says 1767.

> filiam Arcangeli, e [Rosae] Ruggiero** (`3Q9M-CSMY-B79D-B`)

It names four people at once and welds gen 3 to gen 2. Children: **Raffaele 1818** (gen 4 — "cui
imposuit nomen Raphael", godmother Gelsumina Ruggiero); Luigi/Aloysius Dec 1819 (d. 16 Dec 1864);
Pasquale 24 Mar 1830; Antonio 13 May 1843 (m. 23 Jun 1869); **Andrea**, m. Maria Carmela, dau. of
Onofrio & Angela Imbimbo.

## The 1780 date problem — SOLVED

The entry read by machine as **1780** says, in Latin words, *anno … octogesimo nono* — **1789**.
Words beat digits. Pasquale was 22, Chiara 17. The contradiction is closed.

## Historical context (published sources)

- **Arienzo = Argentium, "Terra Argenti"**, the town's own Latin name, used in full in the registers.
  Built on the ruins of **Suessola**, an Oscan city. After a fire in the Lombard–Saracen wars of the
  late 8th c. the Suessolans took refuge on *Mons Argentarium*; the castle there was destroyed by King
  Roger in 1135; they returned to the valley and built the **Terra Murata** in **1154**.
- **Collegiata di Sant'Andrea Apostolo** — documented from **1151**; given the cure of the whole walled
  town in 1585; raised to **collegiate** rank in **1722** with 22 canons and 8 mansionaries.
- **Arienzo was in the diocese of Sant'Agata de' Goti until 1855**, then passed to Acerra with Cervino,
  San Felice a Cancello and Santa Maria a Vico. This is why every Arienzo marriage dispensation in the
  registers is routed through the *Episcopalis Curia Sanctae Agathae Gothorum* — garbled by the OCR as
  "Agache Gochorum".
- **St Alfonso Maria de' Liguori was bishop of that diocese 1762–1775**, and kept his second residence
  **in the palace beside Sant'Andrea at Arienzo**, where he lived for some years because the climate was
  milder — and was criticised by the people of Sant'Agata for it. The building is now the Museo
  Alfonsiano. His health broke down from 1767; he resigned in 1775 and died in 1787.
  **So Pasquale Falco was baptised in the bishop's own church while Liguori held the see and lived next
  door.** He did *not* perform the baptism — the register names the substitute D. Clemens Crisci under
  archpriest Antonio Romano. Say it that way and no further.

## The surnames

- **Falco**: 46,749 bearers worldwide, 12,010th commonest. Two independent origins (Lombard personal
  name; nickname/falconer). Portugal (3,057), Angola (2,958) and the Philippines (1,795) are almost
  certainly a *different* name — Iberian *Falco*/*Falcó* — not Italian emigration.
- **Zampiello**: **~123 bearers worldwide** — 84 USA, **37 Italy**, 2 France; 1,628,521st commonest.
  Effectively a single family, and small enough in Italy to be traced in full.
- **Rivetti** of Arienzo were people of standing — one styled *Doctoris Physici* (physician), members
  given the honorific *Don*.

## The move north was four kilometres, not a migration

Every generation from Matteo to Raffaele is born, married and buried at **Arienzo**. In **1913** a son,
Gerardo, is registered at **Arpaia** — 4 km away, across the provincial line into Benevento. Then
Forchia (1930, 1933, 1938), Benevento (1955), Brisbane. The trail only *looks* broken: the records move
from the Archivio di Stato di Caserta to that of Benevento.

**Forchia** is named for the *Forche Caudine*, where a Roman consular army passed under the yoke in 321 BC.

## Access note

FamilySearch record *images* for several Caserta collections return "This record can only be displayed
on certain accounts" — restricted to FamilySearch centres and affiliate libraries. The *index* and the
*full-text transcriptions* remain reachable, which is what almost all of the above rests on.

## Method that worked, for repeating

See `familysearch-fulltext-method` in project memory. In short:

```
/service/search/fulltext/search?count=50&m.defaultFacets=on&m.queryRequireDefault=on
  &offset=0&c.collectionId=on&f.collectionId=<CID>&q.anyPlace=Arienzo&q.text=<term>
```

`f.collectionId` (with `c.collectionId=on`) is what makes the place filter bind. Collection IDs:
`M9J1-7MY` Campania Religious 1551–1975 · `M9J1-SMK` Campania Deaths · `M9J1-9B4` Campania Marriages ·
`2043630` Caserta SMCV Civil 1866–1929 · `2718545` Caserta Civil 1809–1866 · `2475030` Benevento Civil
1810–1942 · `1483010` Archdiocese of Benevento Church Records 1575–1908.

**Enumerate a whole register** by querying words that appear on every page — *baptizavi*, *infantem*,
*Matrina*, *coniugibus*, *Obstetrix*, *Vicarius* — paging each. That produced 2,059 baptism pages and
1,596 marriage/death pages (11.3 MB of transcription) in `data/arienzo-pages.tsv` and
`data/arienzo-marr-death-pages.tsv`.

**Years are written in Latin words** in the 18th-century entries (*septingentesimo sexagesimo septimo*),
so digit-year search misses them — and where words and digits disagree, trust the words.
