## THE FIRST FALCO FOUND IN THE DEATH SWEEP — 7 March 1836

Page index 11, right-hand act:

> …nel giorno **sette** del mese di **Marzo**, anno corrente, alle ore ventiquattro, **è morta nel
> Monastero d'Ave Gratia Plena, Donna Raffaela de Falco, d'anni sessantasette**, nata in Arienzo,
> di professione **monaca**, domiciliata ivi, figlia di **Don Francesco Falco defunto**,
> di professione **proprietario**, domiciliato ivi.

**Donna Raffaela de Falco — a nun, aged 67, so born about 1769 — died in the monastery of Ave Gratia
Plena at Arienzo on 7 March 1836. Her father was the late Don Francesco Falco, a *proprietario*.**

Three things follow, and the third is a caution:

1. **There was a gentry branch of the Falco at Arienzo.** *Don* Francesco Falco, *proprietario* — a
   landowner with the honorific. Everyone in the documented line is a *contadino*.
2. **The surname is written *de Falco***, with the Campanian prefix. The archive already flagged that
   *Falco* and *De Falco* were used interchangeably by clerks; here is a clerk doing it.
3. **This is not our family, so far as anything shows.** Raffaela was born about 1769, almost exactly
   contemporary with Pasquale Falco (b. 1767) — but a proprietor's daughter who entered religion is a
   different social world from a peasant farmer. **No connection is established, and none should be
   assumed.** Candidates for Don Francesco include the Francesco Falco who married Maria Angela
   Ulivieccio, and the Francesco Falco *Casalis Paulisii* of 1714 — both unproven.

She died in the same house as **Suor Giovanna di Lucia**, the nun recorded in the 1835 register.
