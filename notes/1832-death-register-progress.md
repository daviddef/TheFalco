## The two Falco of 1832

### 1. Giovanna Falco, 70 — act no. 32, image 20 (left), 14 May 1832

> …sono comparsi **Gennaro Crisci**, di anni trenta, di professione contadino, domiciliato in
> **strada Coppa**, vicino della defunta, e **Clemente Crisci**, di anni ventotto… i quali han
> dichiarato, che nel giorno **tredici** del mese di **Maggio**, anno corrente, alle ore ventitre,
> è morta nella propria casa **GIOVANNA FALCO, d'anni settanta**, *moglie di Antonio Sett[embre]*,
> nata in Arienzo, di professione contadina, domiciliata ivi, **figlia di MICHELE FALCO, defunto**,
> di professione contadino, **e di VINCENZA ESPOSITO, defunta**.

Plate: `site/public/plates/giovanna-falco-death-1832.jpg`

**This is the most important thing in the volume.** Giovanna was born about 1762 — five years before
Pasquale Falco. Her father was **Michele Falco**, a *contadino*, married to **Vincenza Esposito**.
Pasquale's father was **Matteo Falco**, a *contadino*, married to **Francesca Crisci**.

So there were **two peasant Falco households at Arienzo in the founding generation**, both fathering
children in the early 1760s. Whether Michele and Matteo were brothers is **not established** — it is
the obvious reading and nothing yet proves it. What it does give is a second, named Falco couple of
that generation, and a plausible origin for the Michele and Michelangelo Falco who appear on strada
Camellara in the 1830s.

### 2. Carmina Falco, 6 months — act no. 80, image 48 (right), 19 December 1832, p. 145

> …sono comparsi **Giovanni Crisci**, di anni trentadue, di professione contadino, domiciliato in
> **strada Camellara**, vicino della defunta, e **Francesco Lettieri**, di anni quaranta, di
> professione contadino, domiciliato in strada Camellara… i quali han dichiarato, che nel giorno
> **diciannove** del mese di **Dicembre**, anno corrente, alle ore due, è morta nella sua propria
> casa **CARMINA FALCO, di mesi sei**, nata in Arienzo, di professione contadina, domiciliata ivi,
> **figlia di FRANCESCO FALCO**, di professione contadino, domiciliato ivi, **e di RAFFAELA FALCO**.

Plate: `site/public/plates/carmina-falco-death-1832.jpg`

**Francesco Falco's wife was herself a Falco.** A Falco married to a Falco — in a town this size that
normally means cousins, and it would have needed a dispensation. This is the same Francesco Falco of
strada Camellara whose ten-month-old daughter **Pasqualina** died on 14 September 1836. His household
is now attested across both ends of the sweep.

## Strada Camellara in 1832
Falco (Francesco & Raffaela), Crisci (Giovanni, Clemente, Marcello, Grazia), Lettieri (Francesco,
Carmine), Torrino (Angelo, Sabatino), Corsi, Carfora, Cognetta, Cioffi, Giglio (Vincenzo, 50, h. Rosa
Gimbardo), Manna, Bruno (Pasquale, 77, *barbiere*), Martone (Maddalena), and —
**Morgillo**: Raffaele Morgillo, 22, son of Carlantonio Morgillo, died 9 May.

That last one matters: **Raffaele Falco (gen 4) married Angela Rosa Morgillo**. The Morgillo the
family married into were living on the same street two generations earlier.

## Also of note
- **Pasquale Vitale, 19, contadino, *nato in Forchia***, died at Arienzo 2 May (img 16 L) — the
  earliest sighting in this archive of Forchia, the town the family moves to a century later.
  Almost certainly unrelated; recorded because the name of the place recurs.
- A great many deaths "nell'Ospedale di Ave Gratia Plena" — the town hospital, which recurs
  throughout as the place the poor and the unattached died.
- A parallel gentry Arienzo runs through the volume: Don Claudio Cimmino (*proprietario*, widower of
  a **Donna Rivetti**), Don Giuseppe de Luptiis, Don Pasquale Crisci (*sacerdote*), Don Nicola
  d'Ambrosio (*medico*, d. 25 Dec, aged 42, unmarried), Donna Serafina de Rogatis. None joined to
  this family.
