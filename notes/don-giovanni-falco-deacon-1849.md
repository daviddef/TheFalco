# DON GIOVANNI FALCO, DEACON — the founder's grandson took orders

**Arienzo, Morti 1849 (`an_ua14351`), atto 66**, image 42, read from the image, 13 September 2026.
Found through the annual death index (`an_ua14309`), which lists him under F as
«**66 · Falco D. Giovanni · Arienzo · Suddiacono · Falco Giuseppe e Guida Antonia**».

---

## Why this one matters

**There has never been a churchman in this line before.** Every Falco the archive has found for four
generations is a *bracciale*, a *contadino* or a *colono* — day-labourers and tenant farmers, all of
them, and most of the declarants at their deaths could not write their own names. **Giovanni Falco
was a DEACON**, in orders, and buried with *Don* before his name.

He is **the founder's grandson**: the son of **GIUSEPPE FALCO and ANTONIA DI GUIDA**, the couple whose
marriage of **4 August 1802** was proved this same day to make Giuseppe a son of **Matteo Falco and
Francesca Crisci**. So the line runs Matteo → Giuseppe → Giovanni, and the third of them reached
the altar.

**Born about 1824** (aged twenty-five in November 1849), **died at twenty-five**, at ten at night, in
his own house on the family's street.

### And the act carries three more facts

1. **GIUSEPPE FALCO WAS STILL ALIVE** in November 1849 — no *fu*, no *defunto* — and is written down
   as a **colono**. He was born about 1780 and married in 1802, so he was about seventy.
2. **STRADA CAMELLARA again.** Both declarants live on it. Three generations of this family are now
   documented on that one street.
3. **Two more of Giuseppe and Antonia's children are known**: the infant **Matteo**, buried 28 August
   1809 at fifteen months, and an infant **Raffaele** whose parish burial is dated 26 February in a
   year not yet read. Giovanni is the third, and the only one to reach adulthood in the record.

## One discrepancy, recorded

The **index** calls him **suddiacono** (subdeacon); the **act** calls him **diacono** (deacon). Both
are written here. If he was ordained deacon shortly before he died, both could be true of different
moments — but that is a guess, and the archive does not know.
