# The 1814 marriage dossier — FOUND, and it was here all along

**Volume**: Arienzo, Processetti matrimoniali 1814 — `an_ua14154`, 290 images
**Bundle**: the pencil folio number **16**, images **265–274**
**Status**: COMPLETE. Read 13 September 2026.

This is the first processetto ever read from the 321-volume holding, and it is the richest
single document this archive has found.

---

## First, a correction

`notes/findings.md` and `notes/QUEUE.md` both said this file did not exist:

> Worked the 1814 processetti (`an_ua14154`, 290 pp.) from the back … So the volume runs to
> **act 25 and then straight into 1815**. … **Act 26's supporting file is not in either.**
> Whether it was never filed, was lost, or sits in an unscanned volume is not established —
> but it is not on Antenati.

> **Dead ends, recorded so they are not repeated** — … Do not search these two again.

**That was wrong, and the reasoning behind it was wrong in an instructive way.**

The bundles in a processetti volume are numbered **by their own sequence, in the order the
banns were published** — not by the number the marriage later received in the marriage
register. Vincenzo and Andreana published their banns in **September** but did not marry until
**15 December**, so their file sits at bundle **16** while their marriage is act **26**.
Bundle 25 — the one the earlier session found at the end of the volume — carries a publication
of **20 November**, two months *later* than ours. The volume was never truncated. The search
was looking for the wrong number.

The lesson is now a standing rule: **in a processetti volume, match on the names and the
publication date, never on the marriage act number.** And a second: a "do not search again"
note is only as good as the reasoning that produced it, and this archive should date and
justify every one it writes.

## THE BIGGEST FIND: Matteo Falco, named in 1814 (image 269, act 85)

Vincenzo was 23 — under the Code's threshold of 25 — so his parents had to consent in person.
They did, before **Felice Morgillo**, notary, on 8 December 1814:

> «…lità ricercate dalla Legge, li conjugi **PASQUALE FALCO del fu MATTEO**, e **CHIARA
> RIVETTI del fu NICOLA**, domiciliati in questa suddetta comune corpo di Arienzo, **strada
> Camellara**, di età maggiore, a me ben conosciuti, i quali li hanno prestato, e prestano il
> loro rispettivo consenso a **Vincenzo Falco loro figlio** nato in costanza del loro legitimo
> Matrimonio, di età maggiore, e con essi domiciliato, acciò lo stesso possa contrarre legitimo
> Matrimonio con **Andreana Crisci figlia di Arcangelo**…»

**This is the earliest document that names Matteo Falco, and the earliest that proves him dead.**

Until now Matteo rested on the baptisms of his children (1767–1786) and on the death acts of
three of them — Angela's of 1822, Rosa's of 1830, Pasquale's of 1834. The earliest proof that
he was dead was Angela's act of **17 October 1822**.

**The bracket on Matteo Falco's death now closes from [1786, Oct 1822] to [1786, 8 Dec 1814]** —
narrowing it by nearly eight years, and from a notarial instrument rather than a register clerk's
recollection.

It also confirms **Chiara Rivetti as daughter of the late Nicola Rivetti**, which until now rested
solely on her 1843 parish burial («*filia quondam Nicolai et Anna*»). Two independent series,
twenty-nine years apart, agree.

## Vincenzo Falco's baptism — the age series is settled (image 266)

The archpriest **Giacinto (Hyacinthus) Morgillo** copied it out of the parish register on
13 September 1814, citing **liber IX baptizatorum, folio 47 a tergo**:

> «…**Anno Domini Millesimo septingentesimo nonagesimo secundo 1792, die vero prima 1
> Februarii**, Ego infrascriptus Archipresbyter baptizavi infantem natum ex **Paschale Falco**,
> et **Clara Rivetta** Conjugibus hujus Parochialis, et Collegialis Ecclesiae Sancti Andreae
> Apostoli, cui dedi nomen **Vincentius Raphael**. Matrina fuit **Caecilia Morone**…»

Two things at once:

1. **Vincenzo Falco was baptised on 1 February 1792**, not born in 1793. The date this archive
   has published for generation three is wrong by a year, and is corrected.
2. **His name at the font was Vincenzo *Raffaele*.** He named his first son Raffaele in 1816,
   and after that child died, named the next Raffaele too. He was passing on his own second name.

### The ages, measured against a real date at last

| Record | Stated | Actual age | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Banns, 11 Sept 1814 | 23 | 22y 7m | in his 23rd year — **right by Italian reckoning** |
| Banns, 18 Sept 1814 | 23 | 22y 7m | **right** |
| Marriage act, 15 Dec 1814 | 21 | 22y 10m | wrong by 2 |
| Raffaele I's birth, 25 Apr 1816 | 35 | 24 | **wrong by 11** |
| Raffaele II's birth, 10 May 1818 | 27 | 26y 3m | in his 27th year — **right** |
| Declaration, 1831 | 38 | 39 | close |
| Death register, 1839 | 42 | 47 | wrong by 5 |
| Death, 15 May 1875 | — | 83 | — |

The famous 21/35/27/44/47/42/37 series was never one man changing his story. **Three of the
statements are exactly right** once you know he was born 1 February 1792 and count the way the
clerks counted — by the year a man is *in*, not the years he has completed. The rest are the
clerks guessing, and the 1816 figure of thirty-five is simply an error.

## Adriana Crisci's baptism — and the Crisci/Cioffi conflict is over (image 268)

Same archpriest, same day, same formula — this one out of **liber IX, folio 94 a tergo**:

> «…**Anno Domini Millesimo septingentesimo nonagesimo sexto 1796, die vero trigesima 30
> Novembris**, R.dus Cl.cus D. **Vincentius Scarpato** Substitutus baptizavit infantem natam ex **Archangelo
> Crisci**, et **Sara Ruggiero** Conjugibus ex mea Parochia, cui dedit nomen **Adriana**. Matrina fuit **Fortunata Villano**.»

- **Born about 30 November 1796** — the first exact date this archive has ever had for her.
  The 1818 birth act put her at 20, implying 1798; she was in fact 21.
- **Her name at the font was ADRIANA.** That is the root of every variant the registers throw
  up — Andreana, Andriana, Andrea, Adriana. They are one woman.
- **Her surname was CRISCI.** The 1839 death register was read here as «Andriana **Cioffi**»,
  and the archive has carried Crisci-vs-Cioffi as an open conflict ever since. It is now closed:
  four documents of 1814 — cover sheet, both consent acts and both banns — plus her own baptism
  extract all say **Crisci**, and name her parents. **The 1839 reading is a clerk's error or
  mine, and the entry stays visible on /corrections rather than being quietly swapped.**

## Two new generations, and a knot

The consent act before notary **Salvio Morgillo** (image 270, act 26) names the bride's side:

> «…li **conjugi ARCANGELO CRISCI fu NICOLA, e SARA RUGGIERO di Pasquale**, domiciliati in
> questa suddetta Comune Corpo, **strada Camellara**, di età maggiore, a me conosciuti… prestano
> il libero rispettivo consenso ad **ANDREANA CRISCI loro figlia**…»

Two names never recorded before:

- **NICOLA CRISCI** — Arcangelo's father, **already dead** by December 1814 (*fu*).
- **PASQUALE RUGGIERO** — Sara's father, **still living** (*di*, not *fu*).

And the Crisci lived on **strada Camellara**, the Falco street. The two families were neighbours
before they were in-laws.

### The knot — and the archive had half of it already

Vincenzo Falco's sister **Anna Falco** (d. 23 August 1820, aged 25) was the wife of an
**Arcangelo Crisci**. Andreana's father is an **Arcangelo Crisci**, of the same street. This
archive has asked before whether that is one man twice married or two men of a name, and the
1817 birth register already answered half of it: **two Arcangelo Crisci declare births in that
single year, one aged 52 and one aged 30**.

**The baptism now decides which one fathered Andreana, by arithmetic.** She was baptised on
30 November 1796, so her father was a married man with a child that year. The elder Arcangelo —
52 in 1817, so born about 1765 — was 31 in 1796. The younger — 30 in 1817, born about 1787 —
was **nine years old**. He cannot be her father.

> **Andreana Crisci's father is the elder Arcangelo Crisci, born about 1765.** That is now fixed.

**What is still not fixed is which of them Anna Falco married.** The archive's standing reading
is the younger, and it remains the simpler one: Anna was born about 1795 and married by about
1819. But the elder cannot be excluded outright — it would need Sara Ruggiero to have died
between December 1814, when she was alive and consenting before a notary, and about 1818.

**The test is Sara Ruggiero's death**, and it is now a specific, dated, falsifiable search rather
than a vague one: the Arienzo death registers, 1815–1818.

## The ages of generation two — published, and awkward

The banns of 11 September 1814 state the parents' ages, and the banns of 18 September repeat
them. This is the **first age ever recorded for Pasquale Falco outside a death act**:

| Person | Stated, Sept 1814 | Implies born | Against the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pasquale Falco** | **60** | c. 1754 | his baptism is **24 Oct 1767** — he was **46**. Wrong by 14. |
| **Chiara Rivetti** | **47** | c. 1767 | her 1843 burial said 80 (b. 1763); the tree says 1772 |
| Vincenzo Falco | 23 | c. 1791 | baptised 1 Feb 1792 — **right** |
| Andreana Crisci | 17 *compiti* | c. 1797 | baptised 30 Nov 1796 — **right**, days off her 18th |

The clerk got both young people right to the year and the father wrong by fourteen. That is the
pattern this archive has been arguing for volumes: **the registers are accurate about the people
standing in front of the clerk and careless about everyone else.** Here the same act proves it
in both directions at once.

Chiara's three estimates — 1763, 1767, 1772 — still do not converge. Her baptism is the next target.

## The officials of Arienzo in 1814

Four Morgillo run the town between them:

- **Vincenzo Morgillo** — *Sindaco*, and officer of the civil state
- **Giacinto Morgillo** — *Archipresbyter* of the collegiate church of Sant'Andrea Apostolo
- **Felice Morgillo**, son of the late Valeriano — public notary, strada Terra Murata
- **Salvio Morgillo** — public notary, strada Terra Murata

Generation four would marry **Angela Rosa Morgillo**, daughter of Orazio, in the next generation.
The family married into the name that ran the comune.

## What this bundle did not contain

Recorded so the search is not repeated:

- **No death certificate for Matteo Falco.** Because Pasquale was alive and consenting in person,
  the dossier needed no certificate for the dead grandfather. The earlier note that "Matteo Falco's
  death act was never going to be attached to this particular marriage" was **right**, and still is.
- No dowry instrument, no *atto di notorietà*, no military discharge.
