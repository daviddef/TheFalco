## Three Falco on the family street, and a Falco household the archive did not have

### Matteo Falco — atto 19, 22 March

> «…sono comparsi **MATTEO FALCO**, di anni **VENTOTTO**, di professione **COLONO**, regnicolo,
> domiciliato ivi **strada CAMELLARA**…»

Plate: `site/public/plates/matteo-falco-1838.jpg`

Born about **1810**. Almost certainly **the later Matteo Falco** the [Crisci](/crisci) page already
carries as husband of **Alessandra Crisci** — and **the first record of his own** this archive has
ever had for him. **He is not the Matteo Falco of 1827**, who was also twenty-eight but a
***possidente*** of strada Porta di Sopra, so born about 1799, and whom this archive has always
called unjoined. **Two men, one name, both twenty-eight in their own act.** Neither is promoted.

### Giuseppe Falco — atto 56, 10 September

> «…sono comparsi **ANDREA CIOFFI**, di anni **SETTANTA** … **strada CAMELLARA**, e **GIUSEPPE
> FALCO**, di anni **CINQUANTA**, di professione **COLONO** … **strada CAMELLARA**…»

Plate: `site/public/plates/giuseppe-falco-1838.jpg`

He is standing for the household of **FRANCESCO CIOFFI** — the family he declared for four times
between 1825 and 1828, and whom the 1828 register calls **«PARENTE DELLA DEFUNTA»**. Ten years on he
is still doing it. **The mother here is Andreana Esposito(?), not the Maria Falco of the 1820s acts**
— a second Francesco Cioffi, or a widower remarried. Not decided.

### Pasquale Falco, thirteen — atto 71, 30 October

> «…è morto nella sua propria casa **PASQUALE FALCO, d'anni TREDICI**, nato in Arienzo, di professione
> contadino, domiciliato ivi, **figlio di FRANCESCO FALCO**, di professione contadino, domiciliato
> ivi, **e di GASPARDA FALCO**, domiciliata ivi.»

Plate: `site/public/plates/pasquale-falco-1838.jpg`

**A Falco household new to this archive** — Francesco Falco and Gasparda Falco, both alive, burying a
son born about **1825**. Two Cioffi of strada Camellara declared it.

**The mother's surname is FALCO.** Either a Falco woman married a Falco man, which in this town is
unremarkable, or the clerk repeated the husband's surname, which these registers also do.
**Not decided, and not treated as a Falco–Falco marriage until another act says so.**

**A correction of my own reading:** at 1800 px I read the boy as *«Raffaele Falco»*. He is
**Pasquale**. The wrong reading is kept because generation four **is** a Raffaele Falco born in 1818,
and a misread Raffaele dying in 1838 would have looked like a catastrophe for the line.

## The ages, and why this archive never dates a birth from one

This volume, set beside 1839, produces the clearest series the sweeps have found.

**VINCENZO FALCO, generation three — every stated age the archive holds:**

| source | date | stated age | implies born |
|---|---|---|---|
| his marriage act | Dec 1814 | **21** | 1793 |
| Raffaele's birth | 1816 | **35** | 1781 |
| Raffaele's birth | 1818 | **27** | 1791 |
| **1838, atto 60** | **Sep 1838** | **47** | **1791** |
| 1839, atto 36 | Mar 1839 | **42** | 1797 |
| 1839, atto 102 | Nov 1839 | **37** | 1801 |

**Forty-seven in September 1838 and thirty-seven fourteen months later.**

**GIUSEPPE FALCO — nine readings:** 46 (1826, *massaro*) · 40 (1827, *colono*) · 56 (1828) ·
**50 (1838, *colono*)** · 53 (Jun 1839, *contadino*) · 53 (Nov 1839, *colono*) · 56 then 53 three days
apart (1841) · 52 (1842). **He is younger in 1838 than he was in 1828.**

**And two neighbours settle the point beyond argument:**

- **ANDREA CIOFFI** of strada Camellara is **seventy** at atto 56 in September 1838 and is buried at
  **eighty-two** six months later (1839, atto 36). **Twelve years in six months.**
- **GAETANO CIOFFI**, *sartore* of strada Camellara, is **fifty-six** at atto 71 in October 1838 and
  dead at **seventy** in November 1839 (1839, atto 98). **Fourteen years in thirteen months.**

Also: Gaspare Giglio 70 / 73 / 72 inside three months; Gennaro Morgillo 40 then 48 nineteen days
apart; Domenico Morgillo 30 here and 28 in 1839; Giuseppe Mastandrea 76 here and 70 in 1839; the
Lettieri brothers moving eight years in nine months.

> **CORRECTED, 10 September 2026.** This file originally offered **Antonio Bernardo** as *«the useful
> counter-case — 70, 76, 70, 70 across four acts, three of them agreeing»*, and argued from him that
> the ages are not random noise. **The 1837 volume then produced him at FIFTY-THREE** (atto 52,
> June 1837, read at 3400 px) — eight months before the first of those seventies, and on a different
> street. **Either there are two Antonio Bernardo, both *possidenti*, or one man aged seventeen years
> in eight months.** The counter-case is withdrawn. It is left standing here, struck through by this
> note rather than deleted, **because the argument was published and the correction is the point.**

**The ages are what a man says and a clerk writes, and they are wrong often enough that nothing may
rest on them.**

## The most useful mistake in the volume — atto 77

At 1800 px I read a seventy-year-old woman as **«MARIA CARFORA»** — **the single name this archive
most wants to find**, the probable mother of Filomena Annecchino and the hinge of the whole
Annecchino line.

At 3400 px it dissolved:

> «…è morta nella sua propria casa **MARIA CARBONE, d'anni SETTANTA, vedova di SAVERIO CARFORA**,
> nata in Arienzo … **figlia di ANIELLO CARBONE, Defunto** … **e di ANGELA COSSI(?), Defunta**.»

Plate: `site/public/plates/maria-carbone-1838.jpg`

**She is a CARBONE. The Carfora was her husband.** *Carbone* and *Carfora* differ by two letters in a
hand that joins everything, and **the name a researcher is hunting is the name their eye supplies**.
Unchecked it would have put a Maria Carfora born about **1768** into the Arienzo register — a century
and a province away from Forchia — and someone, some day, would have joined her to generation seven.

**Nothing in this volume touches Filomena Annecchino's mother.** That question is still open and the
act that would close it is still the unindexed **Forchia birth register for 1897**.

## What it gave the surrounding families

**MAIONE — seven households in one year.** Margarita (atto 5), Teresa(?) (12), **Gennaro, a
*COLONO*** (47), Saverio (61), **Lucrezia, wife of Pietro Zimbardo** (70), and **Carmine with Antonio
declaring** (87). The Zimbardo–Majone household runs straight into the next volume: they bury a
four-year-old here on 20 October 1838 and a thirteen-month-old on 6 October 1839. **None is joined to
Costanza Maione.**

**MORGILLO pushed back to about 1720 — atto 24.**

> «…è morto nella sua propria casa **ANGELO MORGILLO, d'anni NOVANTA, vedovo di MARIA RIVETTI**, nato
> in Arienzo, di professione **COLONO** … **figlio di GENNARO MORGILLO, Defunto** … **e di MADDALENA
> D'AMBROSIO Defunta**.»

Plate: `site/public/plates/angelo-morgillo-1838.jpg`

Born about **1748**, so his father **Gennaro Morgillo** about **1720** — the earliest Morgillo the
archive can name. **And his wife was a RIVETTI.** Morgillo and Rivetti are the two surnames that
flank this line, and here they are married to each other a generation before the founding couple's
children. **Nothing joins Maria Rivetti to Chiara**, whose parents the 1843 act names as Nicola
Rivetti and Anna di Ruggio. *(At 1800 px I read the age as ninety-two and the wife as «Marta Grieco».
Both were wrong; both are corrected from the same plate rather than replaced.)*

A second deep Morgillo at atto 63: **Crescenza Morgillo, 70**, daughter of **Sabato Morgillo**, so
another Morgillo father of the 1740s.

**CARFORA on strada Camellara.** **Antonio Carfora and Giuseppa Pesce(?) bury two children in one
year** — Maria Rosa at four months in January (atto 9) and **Clemente at four years** in July (atto
42) — both declared by men of the Falco street. **A Carfora household on Camellara itself** is the
nearest that surname has come to this family's door. And atto 21 buries **AGNESE CARFORA, ninety**,
born about **1748**, daughter of a Giuseppe Carfora of about 1720. **Still nothing joins any of them
to Maria Carfora of Forchia.**

**FERRARO with a father named — atto 66.** **Maria Ferraro, 28**, wife of Giuseppe Sepistella,
**figlia di FRANCESCO FERRARO**, contadino, living. Born about 1810. The [Ferrara](/ferrara) page
turns on the family writing *Ferrara* and the clerks writing **FERRARO** — and the Arienzo register
writes Ferraro too, ninety years before Giuseppa. **It is not a link to Giuseppa Ferrara of Arpaia.**

**And *ferraro* as a trade, four pages away** — Biagio Ruggiero, 40, *ferraro*, at atto 88. **One
word, two meanings, in one volume.** It is the collision that had the register builder filing
Giuseppa Ferraro under her middle name until it was fixed; the registers themselves are why the fix
works, because a trade follows a comma and a surname does not.

## The town in 1838

**A soldier's daughter, spinning wool — atto 73.**

> «…**MARIA ELISABETTA BAUMGARTEN, d'anni QUARANTASETTE**, nata in **NAPOLI**, di professione
> **FILATRICE**, domiciliata in **strada Croce**, **figlia di Don GIUSEPPE BAUMGARTEN, Defunto**, di
> professione **MILITARE** … **e di Donn'ANDREANA TORINO, Defunta**.»

Plate: `site/public/plates/baumgarten-1838.jpg`

Born about **1791 in Naples**, dead poor in a hill town at forty-seven. The Bourbon army of Naples
kept German regiments and recruited German officers, and a *Don Giuseppe Baumgarten, militare*, in
Naples about 1790 fits that world exactly — **but this archive has not verified it and does not
assert it.**

**A stranger dead in an inn — atto 30.** **Alessandro del Vecchio, 40, «nato in MELIZZANO»**, died
**in an *osteria***, not a house.

**Three generations of the gentry Lettieri.** Atto 11 buries **Donna Angela Maria Lettieri, 38,
*proprietaria*, «figlia di Don Vincenzo Lettieri»**; atto 39, five months later, buries **Don Vincenzo
Lettieri himself, 76, *possidente***, and names *his* father, **Don Emilio(?) Lettieri**.
**The daughter's act is what makes the father's identifiable.**

**Two Bonza infants styled «GENTILUOMO»** — nine months old at atto 69, two months at atto 85. **The
register gives a two-month-old a rank.**

**A household taken apart in twelve days.** Atto 43 buries **Domenico Cioffi, 32**, on 1 August; atto
49 buries his **ten-month-old daughter** on the 13th. **Francesca Capasso lost her husband and her
baby inside a fortnight**, and it takes reading the two acts together to see it.

**A third surname running in two worlds.** Atto 45 buries **il Signor Domenico Giglio, *possidente***,
son of a *possidente* Gaspare Giglio — while atti 4, 18 and 21 have **Gaspare Giglio, *contadino* of
strada Camellara**, in his seventies. Same name, two ranks — the pattern already published for
[Rivetti](/rivetti) and the Morgillo. **The archive's Giglio are the labouring ones.**

**A date that could not exist.** At 1800 px I read atto 15 as «il dì **ventinove** Febbrajo». **1838
was not a leap year**, and the arithmetic — not a second opinion — caught it. At 3200 px the word is
not *ventinove*; the neighbouring acts bracket it to **24 February**. **A date that cannot exist is
the cheapest error-detector this archive has, and it only works if the failures are written down.**

> Read at 3400 px: «…è morto nella sua propria casa **ANGELO MORGILLO, d'anni NOVANTA, vedovo di
> MARIA RIVETTI**, nato in Arienzo, di professione **COLONO**, domiciliato ivi, **figlio di GENNARO
> MORGILLO, Defunto**, di professione colono, domiciliato ivi, **e di MADDALENA D'AMBROSIO Defunta**.»

> earliest Morgillo this archive can name, and a whole generation behind the Monticello and San

> **Nothing joins Maria Rivetti to Chiara**, whose parents the 1843 act names as Nicola Rivetti and
> Anna di Ruggio. They are of roughly one generation and one small town, which is a reason to look,
> not a reason to claim. **It is recorded because it is true, not because it means anything yet.**

> both are corrected here from the same plate rather than quietly replaced.

> ### Atto 77 — the correction worth publishing above all the others
>
> At 1800 px I read this woman as **«MARIA CARFORA»** and my hand went straight to the zoom, because
> **Maria Carfora is the single name this archive most wants to find** — the probable mother of
> Filomena Annecchino, and the hinge of the whole [Annecchino](/annecchino) line.
>
> At 3400 px it dissolved. The act reads:
>
> «…è morta nella sua propria casa **MARIA CARBONE, d'anni SETTANTA, vedova di SAVERIO CARFORA**,
> nata in Arienzo, di professione contadina, domiciliata ivi, **figlia di ANIELLO CARBONE, Defunto**,
> di professione contadino, domiciliato ivi, **e di ANGELA COSSI(?), Defunta**.»
>
> **She is a CARBONE. The Carfora was her husband.**
>
> **This is left here in full because it is the most useful mistake in the volume.** The name a
> researcher is hunting is the name their eye will supply, and *Carbone* and *Carfora* differ by two
> letters in a hand that joins everything. Had it gone unchecked it would have put a seventy-year-old
> Maria Carfora into the Arienzo register — a woman born about **1768**, a hundred years and a
> province away from Forchia — and someone, some day, would have joined her to generation seven.
>
> **Nothing in this volume touches Filomena Annecchino's mother.** That question is still open, and
> the act that would close it is still the unindexed **Forchia birth register for 1897**.
