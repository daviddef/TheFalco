# Arienzo civil death register 1829 — sweep complete

**Volume:** `an_ua14331` (Arienzo, Morti 1829), 65 images. Read in full.
**Acts:** 2 January – 29 December 1829. The volume opens with the printed frontispiece of the
*Regno delle Due Sicilie* and, as the earlier note in this archive said, runs death acts to the end.
**Duplicate bindings:** 13≡12, 16≡15, 22≡21, 30≡29, 45≡44, 49≡48, 53≡52, 55≡54, 59≡58, 61≡60, 64≡63.

## The result: no Falco at all

**Not one Falco death is recorded in the Arienzo civil register for 1829.** Sixty-five images, a full
year of acts, and the surname does not appear once — not as a deceased, not as a parent, not as a
declarant.

This is the only year of the seven now read that contains no Falco whatever.

### Why a null is worth recording
1. **It is consistent with Pasquale Falco being alive.** He died on 1 October 1834. Nothing in 1829
   contradicts that, and the archive's bracket held.
2. **It raises a discrepancy worth checking.** This archive has carried the statement that Pasquale is
   *"named again without* quondam *in his daughter Angela's death entry of 1829"* — a reading taken
   from the **parish** register through FamilySearch's full text. A death at Arienzo in 1829 should
   normally appear in **both** the parish book and the civil register. **There is no Angela Falco, and
   no Falco of any name, in the 1829 civil register.**

   Three explanations are possible and this archive does not choose between them: the parish entry may
   be of a different year; the name or surname may have been misread in the machine transcription; or
   the death was recorded by the priest and never carried to the *stato civile*. **The parish entry
   should be re-read against the image before it is relied on again.** Logged as an open question.

## What the year does show
The register is dominated by child mortality — of roughly 120 acts, well over half are children under
five, and several households lose more than one. **Three Gimbardo infants of strada Carpaneta** die
within days of each other in November (Giuseppe, one day; Angela, one day; Anna, three days), which
reads as a multiple birth that did not survive.

**Strada Camellara** appears throughout as the address of the deceased or the declarants — Crisci
(Gaetano, Gennara, Arcangelo), Giglio (Maria, 29), Liparulo, Laudato, Ferrara, di Lugo — but with no
Falco among them this year.

**Arcangelo Crisci** appears as the father of a one-day-old son, Gaetano, on strada Camellara on
5 May 1829. This bears on the archive's standing question about *two* Arcangelo Crisci — the man named
as Andreana Crisci's father, and the man who married Anna Falco and was widowed in 1820. A man of that
name fathering a child in 1829 is compatible with a widower's remarriage. **Compatible, not proof.**

## Also of note
- Two children of a **Giordano** family, both **born at Catanzaro** in Calabria, die at Arienzo in
  July and August — incomers from four hundred kilometres south.
- **Donna Elisabetta Capaccio**, a *gentildonna* **born at Aversa**; **Alessandro Saccaniello**, born
  at **Santa Maria a Vico**; **Giuseppa Giordano**, born at **Catanzaro** — the register catches the
  town's incomers precisely because it asks where everyone was born.
- Two nuns of the Ave Gratia Plena die in the monastery: **Donna Carmela Romano**, 78, daughter of
  Don Gaetano Romano, and earlier **Donna Concetta de Caso**, aged eight, called *gentildonna*.
