# «Quondam» is not evidence of death in this parish — the pair that proves it

**14 September 2026.** This archive has reasoned, in at least seven records and on two pages, that
**a parent named WITHOUT «quondam» (Latin) or «fu» (Italian) was alive** on the date of the act. For
the **parish Latin register of Arienzo** that inference is now **disproved**, not merely doubted.

## The three acts, one register, one hand, four weeks

| date | act | what the scribe wrote |
|---|---|---|
| **12 Jul 1802** | Rosa Falco m. Carlo Antonio Morgillo | «*Carolum Antonium Morgillo filium **q.** Caroli Antonii*» — dead father, **marked** |
| **18 Jul 1802** | **Matteo Falco dies**, aged «*annorum 55 circiter*» | margin «*Mattheus Falco*» |
| **4 Aug 1802** | Giuseppe Falco m. Antonia di Guida | «*non dum uxoratos **Josephum Falco filium Matthæi**, e Franciscæ Crisci*» — **no «q.», seventeen days after the burial** |

All three are the work of **Giacinto Morgillo**, archpriest. Both marriage readings are **from the
image, at magnification**; so is the death act.

So the same scribe, in the same book, within four weeks, **marked one dead father and not the other**.
He was not following a rule. He was writing.

## Why this matters more than one date

The absence of «quondam» was being used as *positive evidence of life* — an argument from silence in
which the silence had never been tested. It has now been tested against a man whose burial is
recorded **on the same film**, and it failed.

**What this does and does not overturn:**

- **Parish Latin acts** — the inference is dead. «*filium Matthæi*» tells you the father's name and
  nothing at all about whether he was breathing.
- **Civil acts** (Antenati, and the Italian series) — **not directly impeached.** A clerk writing
  «*figlia del fu Crescenzo Rivetti, ed Angela Falco*» is completing a form whose fields demand the
  status of each parent, not choosing a word. Those inferences are probably sound.
- **But none of them is free any more.** Every «named without «fu», so alive» in this archive is now
  carried as a **weak inference**, flagged in the record itself, and **none may stand alone against a
  document.**

## It was already half-suspected

The open-questions page had a live doubt of exactly this shape — Angela Falco's death of 1829, where
Pasquale Falco appears without «quondam» and was taken to be alive. That entry now says the reasoning
is disproved as a class rather than doubted in one instance.

## The other thing the same read fixed

Matteo Falco's death act, `3Q9M-CSMY-B744-5`, has anchored this archive for months while its own
citation admitted «**image not read**». It is read now:

> «Anno D̄ni Millesimo octingentesimo secundo **1802**, die vero **decima octava 18 Julii**,
> **Mattheus Falco vir Franciscæ Crisci**, aetatis suae annorum **55 circiter**, omnibus Sacramentis
> munitus, in Comm. S. M. E. obiit, et in Archipresbyterali Coemeterio ejus cadaver humatum est.»
> — Hyacinthus Archipr. Morgillo

**The 55 is confirmed**, so b. c.1747 stands. And it says «**vir**», not «*viduus*» — Francesca Crisci
outlived him, which her own act of 31 December 1805 confirms.

**A citation that records its own weakness is doing its job.** The note «image not read» is the only
reason this was checked at all, four months after it was first published.
