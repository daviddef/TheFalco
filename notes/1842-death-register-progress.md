## What this volume has already given

### It corrects a hypothesis the 1843 file published — atto 2, 2 January

> …è morto nella sua propria casa **LUIGI CIOFFI di un giorno**, nato in Arienzo, di professione
> contadino, domiciliato ivi, **figlio di RAFFAELE CIOFFI** di professione contadino, domiciliato
> ivi, **e di FRANCESCA FALCO**, domiciliata ivi.

The 1843 sweep read the wife of Raffaele Cioffi, at strip resolution, as *«Domenica Falco»*, and
**published it as a hypothesis rather than a finding, precisely because it had not been enlarged**.
This act, read at 2600px, gives the forename as **FRANCESCA**. The 1843 file now carries the
correction beside the error rather than in place of it.

**Raffaele Cioffi and Francesca Falco lost two infants in fourteen months** on strada Cioffi — Luigi
at one day old, 2 January 1842; Antonio at seventeen days, 10 June 1843. Both parents are named
without *«fu»* in both acts.

**She is not the Francesca Falco the archive already had.** That one is the wife of **Gioacchino**
Cioffi, mother of Aniello Cioffi who died at seventy-six in 1843 — so born in the 1740s. This one is
bearing children in 1842. Two women, two generations apart, each married to a Cioffi. Neither one's
parents are named by any act yet, and neither is guessed at.

### A Morgillo elder with a father's name — atto 7

> …è morta nella sua propria casa **ANGELA MORGILLO**, di anni **ottanta**, *vedova di Gaspare
> Ummarino*, nata in Arienzo, contadina, **figlia di SIMONE MORGILLO defunto**, contadino, **e di
> VITTORIA DIGLIO defunta**.

Born about **1762**. A **Simone Morgillo** is one of four brothers — with Biagio, Antonio and
Domenico — in the San Gaetano household the archive built out of the 1828 volume. Same name, same
town, right generation. **Not proven to be the same man, and not merged.**

**Three different Angela Morgillo are now in play** and the archive is keeping them apart:
the wife of **Nunzio Lettieri**, alive in January 1842 (atto 4); this **widow of Gaspare Ummarino**,
dead in February 1842; and the *«fu Angela Morgillo»* who is Nicodemo Cimmino's mother in 1843
atto 3. The second and third *could* be one woman remarried — the dates allow it — and that is a
possibility, not a claim.

Counting **Teresa Morgillo** (atto 12) and **Giovanni Morgillo**, 55, a declarant on strada Cioffi
(atto 11), this volume already names five Morgillo across at least four households, none of them
yet connected to Angela Rosa Morgillo who married Raffaele Falco.

### The archpriest of Arienzo — atto 15, 26 February

> …è morto nella sua propria casa **DON'ANGELO SCARPATI di anni SETTANTASEI**, nato in Arienzo,
> **di professione ARCIPRETE**, domiciliato ivi, **figlio del Signor STEFANO SCARPATI defunto**, di
> professione **possidente**, e **della Signora FAUSTINA CIMMINO defunta**.

Born about **1766**, dead on 26 February 1842. This archive has been reading Latin parish burial
entries from Arienzo for months; **the archpriest through most of that period now has a name, an
age and a family** — the son of a propertied house, not a labouring one.

What it does *not* establish: that he personally wrote any particular entry. An archpriest presides
over a parish; curates write. The entries of his tenure were made under his authority, and that is
as far as the record goes. Chiara Rivetti's 1843 burial entry was written after his death.

### A stranger dead at the inn — atto 16, 27 February

> …è morto **nella locanda di NICOLA CIOFFI**, **Don DOMENICO RICCI**, di anni **quarantasei**,
> **nato in MAGLIANO**, di professione ***corattiere***, domiciliato in questo comune…

And then the form's two parent lines are **left blank**. Not «defunto», not a guess — **nothing**.
A livestock dealer from out of town died in an Arienzo inn, and the two men who reported it, one of
them the innkeeper, could not name his mother or his father.

**The blank is published as a blank.** It is the clearest thing in the volume about how these
registers actually worked: they record what the neighbours knew, and a stranger has no neighbours.

### The hospital and the monastery were the same house

Two deaths here happen *«nell'Ospedale d'Ave Grazia Piena»* — **Sabatino Lopa, 70** (atto 3) and
**Cesare Giglio, 13** (atto 17). In 1843 two nuns die *«nel monastero di Ave Grazia Piena»*. The
**Ave Gratia Plena** house at Arienzo held both a convent and a hospital, and the register uses the
two words for the two parts of it.

Both hospital deaths were declared by **the same two men** — Angelo Diglio, a *contadino* **of the
comune of San Felice a Cancello**, and Nicola Migliore, both of *strada dell'Ospedale*. The 1843
monastery deaths were likewise declared by a fixed pair, Salvatore Lettieri and Gaetano Sposato of
strada Annunziata. **Institutional deaths in Arienzo went through designated neighbours, not
through the institution.** That is a finding about the record-keeping, and it is worth knowing
before anyone reads a declarant's presence as a sign of kinship.

### A Falco on strada Camellara in 1842 — atto 41, 20 July

> …sono comparsi **VINCENZO CIOFFI**, di anni quarantasei, di professione ***vinivendolo***,
> regnicolo, domiciliato in strada **CAMELLARA**, **e GIUSEPPE FALCO, di anni CINQUANTADUE, di
> professione COLONO**, regnicolo, **domiciliato in strada CAMELLARA**…

Plate: `site/public/plates/giuseppe-falco-1842.jpg`

A new sighting of Giuseppe Falco, on the line's own street, in the trade the register always gives
him. **It sharpens the age problem instead of solving it.** The run is now:

| 1817 | 1820s | 1828 | **Jul 1842** | May 1843 | Aug 1843 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 37 | 40 · 46 · 46 · 40 | 56 | **52** | 56 | 55 |

**Fifty-two, then fifty-six ten months later.** Two numbers ten months apart that differ by four
years are not two measurements of one man, and they are not two men either — the street, the trade
and the name are the same. They are a clerk's guess, twice. The archive publishes the series and
still declines to pick a birth year out of it.

**And a third Cioffi is now on strada Camellara.** Luca Cioffi (*sartore*) and Giovanni Cioffi
(*contadino*) reported Chiara Rivetti's death there in September 1843; Vincenzo Cioffi, a
*vinivendolo*, stands beside Giuseppe Falco here in July 1842. The Falco and the Cioffi were
neighbours on that street across both years. **What the acts do not say is that they were kin** —
the 1828 register says *parente* of a Falco declarant when it means it, and it does not say it here.

### The lay sisters of the Ave Gratia Plena came from out of town

Four women of that house are now in the registers, and the pattern is sharp:

| act | name | rank | born |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1842 atto 29 | **Maria Lopa**, 45 | *conversa* | **Forchia d'Arpaia** |
| 1842 atto 37 | **Domenica di Guida**, 42 | *conversa* | **Santa Maria a Vico** |
| 1843 atto 57 | **Giulia Migliore**, 80 | *conversa* | Arienzo |
| 1843 atto 62 | **Donna Maria Giuseppa Calabria**, 80 | *monaca*, daughter of a *proprietario* | Arienzo |

The *converse* — the lay sisters, who did the work — are the ones who came from elsewhere, and their
fathers are *contadini* and *villani*. The one *monaca* is local gentry. **The class line ran
straight through the cloister wall, and so did the map.** Forchia and Arpaia are the towns this
family's other lines come from, which is worth noting and is not, by itself, a connection.

> **A correction this table forced.** The 1843 file first called the older lay sister *«Suor **Elena**
> Giulia Migliore»*. There is no Elena. The clerk writes *«morta nel monastero di Ave Gratia **Plena**
> — [name]»* in one unbroken line, and the word taken for a forename is the house's own. Atto 62
> settles it — there the same word is followed by *«**Donna** Maria Giuseppa Calabria»* — and 1842
> repeats the construction twice. The 1843 file now carries the correction.
