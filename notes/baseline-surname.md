# Falco — baseline surname research

Established 2026-09-07, before the MyHeritage tree was loaded. Nothing here is about
David's line specifically; it is the backdrop against which that line has to be placed.

## What the name is

*Falco* is Latin/Italian for **falcon**. It reaches modern Italy by two independent routes,
and this matters for method:

1. **As a Lombard (Longobard) personal name.** *Falco* was a common given name among the
   Lombards. Where it survives as a surname by this route it is a **patronymic** — the family
   of a man called Falco — not a nickname.
2. **As a nickname or occupational name** — for someone quick, sharp-sighted or predatory,
   or for a *falconiere*, a falconer, an occupation of real standing in the medieval and
   Norman-Sicilian nobility.

**Consequence for the archive:** as with *De Franceschi*, there is **no single founding Falco
family**. The name was coined independently many times over, in many places. Any claim that two
Falco lines are connected has to be earned from documents, not assumed from the name.

**Read the Iberian numbers with care.** Portugal (3,057) and Angola (2,958) are almost certainly a
*separate* name — Portuguese/Galician *Falco* and the Catalan *Falcó* (116 bearers listed as a
variant) — carried to Angola by Portuguese colonial settlement, not Italian emigration. The
Philippines cluster (1,795) is likewise most plausibly Spanish-colonial. Do not fold these into
an Italian story without evidence.

The genuinely Italian diaspora is **USA, Argentina, Brazil, France, Uruguay, Venezuela, Canada,
Australia** — the standard late-19th/early-20th-century emigration fan.

## Distribution — Italy

Cognomix: **c. 7,826 Falco families** in Italy. A separate estimate gives c. 3,000 *nuclei*;
the two are counting different things and neither is a census, so treat both as indicative only.

Regional split (paginainizio): **South and islands 59.8%**, north 32%, centre 8.1%.
Named strongholds: **Sicily** (Palermo, Catania, Messina), **Calabria**, **Campania**.

The north's 32% is large enough that it cannot be dismissed as diaspora-within-Italy, but it may
substantially be 20th-century internal migration to Milan and Turin rather than an old northern
population. **This needs testing against pre-1900 records, not accepted.**

## The variant family — and why the prefix matters

| Form | Where it dominates |
|---|---|
| **Falco**, and the patronymics **De Falco** / **Di Falco** | South |
| **Falcone**, **Falconio**, **Falconieri** | South |
| **Falchi**, **Falchieri** | Emilia (and Sardinia, for Falchi) |
| **Falcoz** | Valle d'Aosta |
| **Falconèr** | the Venetie |

**De Falco is strongly Campanian** — Naples and its province above all. If David's line proves to
be Campanian, the *Falco* / *De Falco* boundary will be the first thing to test in the registers,
because clerks moved between the two forms freely and a line can appear under both.

## Open questions this baseline cannot answer

- Which region is David's line from? Everything above forks on that answer.
- Is the name patronymic (Lombard) or a nickname in his line?
- Is *Falco* the original form, or a clipping of *De Falco* / *Falcone* by a registrar or at
  a port of arrival?
