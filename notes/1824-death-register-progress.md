# Arienzo civil death register 1824 — sweep complete

> **CORRECTION (published, not swapped).** Where this file reads **«Cossi»** for the household
> of **Francesco Cossi and Maria Falco of strada Camellara**, and for the **Cesare** and
> **Marcello** who declare with Giuseppe Falco on that street, the register actually reads
> **CIOFFI**. The 1825 volume distinguishes the two families in one hand: *Cioffi* with a
> dotted «i» after the C, *Cossi* — of strada Cossi — without. See
> `site/public/plates/cioffi-not-cossi-1825.jpg`. The text below is left as it was read.

## PASQUALE FALCO ACTS FOR HIMSELF — atto 8, 25 March 1824

> …sono comparsi **PASQUALE FALCO**, di anni **cinquantacinque**, di professione **COLONO**,
> regnicolo, domiciliato in questo comune, **strada Camellara**, *vicino del defunto*, e **GIUSEPPE
> FALCO**, di anni **quarantadue**, di professione **colono**, regnicolo, domiciliato ivi **strada
> Camellara**, *vicino del defunto*…

Plate: `site/public/plates/pasquale-falco-declares-1824.jpg`

**This is the only record in which the ancestor acts rather than being spoken of.** Everywhere else
Pasquale Falco is named by somebody else — on his daughter Anna's act of 1820, on his own death act
of 1834. Here he walks into the town hall with his brother and reports a neighbour's five-year-old
dead.

It gives two things the archive did not have:

- **his trade as *colono***, a tenant farmer, where the 1834 act calls him *contadino*;
- **his age as fifty-five**, one year out against the baptism of 24 October 1767 — ordinary here.

And it is **the first act found in which the two brothers appear together**.

The dead child's mother is the third **MARIA FALCO**, wife of Francesco Cossi — the same household
that lost a daughter in June 1823. **Two doubts recorded rather than smoothed over:** the surname can
be read *Cossi* or *Crisci* in this hand, and the household is given here on **strada Camellara**
where the 1823 act put it on strada Sant'Andrea.

## DONN'ANNA QUOTOLO — atto 20, 25 April 1824

> …**è morta nella sua propria casa DONN'ANNA QUOTOLO di anni OTTANTOTTO, vedova di DON NICOLA
> RIVETTI**, nata in Arienzo, di professione **GENTILDONNA**, **figlia di SILVESTRO QUOTOLO
> Defunto**, di professione **POSSIDENTE**, e di Maria d'Isteria(?) Defunta.

Plate: `site/public/plates/donna-anna-quotolo-death-1824.jpg`

**This is one half of the gentry couple that has been a candidate for Chiara Rivetti's parents since
1816.** Until now she existed only as a name on her daughter Francesca's act. Now she has her own
death, her age — **born about 1736**, so twenty-seven when Chiara was born, which fits — and her
father, **Silvestro Quotolo, *possidente***.

The class objection stands, but it is a little softer than it was: **she died on strada Croce
dell'Ospedale with two *contadini* Migliore for neighbours**, not in a palace. The open-questions
page now sets out both couples with this act added.

**One correction to my own reading.** At low resolution I read her surname as *Pinotolo*. Enlarged,
it is plainly **Quotolo**, matching the 1816 act.

## No Falco died in 1824

Eighty-five acts. The surname appears only in atto 8 — twice as declarant, once as a mother.

## The Rivetti keep multiplying, and keep refusing to sort

This volume adds: **Antonio Rivetti** (strada Via Nuova), **Fabrizio Rivetti** (*vaticale*, strada
Camellara), **Gennaro Rivetti** (strada Camellara), **Giuseppe Rivetti** (45, strada Camellara),
**Angela Rivetti** (80, daughter of a Giuseppe Rivetti), **Maria Rivetti** (75, daughter of a
Carmine Rivetti, married **Angelo Morgillo**), **Salvatore Rivetti** (58, son of the late Gennaro,
married Anna Gimbardo), **Angelo Rivetti** (28, *calzolajo*), **Giovanni Rivetti** (*possidente*),
and **Don Giuseppe Rivetti, *sacerdote***, declaring twice.

**Francesca delli Paoli**, seventy, wife of **Paolo Rivetti** of Monticello de' Cappuccini, died in
October — the mother of the Vincenzo Rivetti who died in January 1823. **The priest declared her
death**, which is the fourth time he acts for that labouring house.

## The di Guida of strada Maddalena now include a possidente

**Raffaele di Guida** (48) and **Gennaro di Guida** (40), *contadini*; and **LUIGI DI GUIDA**,
twenty-seven, a ***possidente***, with **Simone di Guida**, twenty-six. The family Giuseppe Falco
married into runs, like the Rivetti and the Quotolo, from labourers to property.

## Two deaths that are not ordinary

- **A body found.** On 20 September **DOMENICO IULIANO of FORCHIA D'ARPAIA** *«si è rinvenuto
  morto»* — was found dead — in the place called **La Maddalena**. Forchia and Arpaia are the towns
  this family's Annecchino and Zampiello come from; a man of Forchia dying on Arienzo ground is the
  first crossing of those two geographies the archive has seen in a register.
- **The mayor.** **DON VINCENZO MORGILLO**, seventy, ***proprietario***, husband of Donn'Orsola,
  died on **19 November 1824**. Vincenzo Morgillo had signed every act in this register since 1820,
  and from 3 November the acts are signed by the *secondo eletto* instead. **The act does not say he
  was the Sindaco**, so the identification is an inference — but a strong one.

## Trades and institutions new to the archive

A ***tessitrice*** (weaver), a ***ragioniere*** (accountant), two ***sacrista*** (sacristans), an
***oste*** (innkeeper), a ***legale*** (lawyer) from the Province of Avellino, and a man described
by a word this reading takes as ***Ebreo*** — that last is **not certain** and is written down as
read. **Three nuns** of the **Monastero di Ave Grazia Plena** died this year, from Naples, from
Caserta and from Arienzo, which shows the house drew women from well beyond the town.
