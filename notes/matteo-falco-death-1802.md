# MATTEO FALCO DIED ON 18 JULY 1802

**Arienzo parish death register, ark `3Q9M-CSMY-B744-5`.** Margin: «**Mattheus Falco**».

> «**Anno Domini Millesimo octingentesimo secundo 1802, die vero decimo octavo 18 Julii, MATTHAEUS
> FALCO VIR FRANCISCAE CRISCI, aetatis suae annorum 55 circiter**, omnibus Sacramentis munitus in
> Comm[unione] S. M. E. **obiit**, et **in Archip[resbyter]ali Coemeterio ejus cadaver humatum est**.
> **Hyacinthus Archip[resbyte]r Morgillo**»

> **READ FROM THE IMAGE, 13 September 2026.** The margin carries his name and a cross. The hand on
> this leaf is unusually clear.

## HE WAS ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE — so he was born about 1747

«**aetatis suae annorum 55 circiter**». This archive has carried **«born about 1740»** since the
beginning, from nothing better than the date of his first known child.

**Fifty-five fits everything.** He fathered children from **1767 to 1786** — from twenty to thirty-nine
— and he married by 1766, at about nineteen. The 1740 guess made him twenty-seven at his first child
and forty-six at his last, which was never impossible and was never evidence either.

*The figure is written «55». In this hand the two glyphs are identical to each other; the second could
be an 8. **About fifty-five, and born about 1744–1747**, is what the archive will say.*

---

## The question this closes

Matteo Falco is the head of this line. **Finding his death has been the archive's central open
question for months.** It has produced a chapter of negatives: six civil death indexes read page for
page, a 703-entry sweep of a parish collection, a note that «he died outside the civil series», and —
this afternoon — a seven-year window argued from the absence of a single Latin word.

**He died on 18 July 1802, and he is buried in the archpriest's own cemetery at Sant'Andrea
Apostolo.**

## AND IT REFUTES THE ARGUMENT THIS ARCHIVE PUBLISHED FOUR HOURS AGO

This afternoon this archive argued, at length and with evidence, that **Matteo Falco was alive on
4 August 1802**, because his son Giuseppe's marriage act of that date calls him «**filium MATTHEI, et
FRANCISCAE CRISCI**» with **no *quondam***, and because the same scribe writes «filium **quondam**
Caroli Antonii» on the very same leaf.

**He had been dead for seventeen days.**

> **The scribe did not use *quondam* for Matteo Falco, and Matteo Falco was dead.** The reasoning was
> careful, it was published with its warrant attached, it was labelled strong evidence rather than
> proof — **and it was wrong.**

**That is the finding, not a footnote to it.** An argument from silence in these registers is worth
less than this archive just assumed, even when the scribe's own practice appears to support it. The
conclusion it produced — «died between August 1802 and 1809» — was **wrong at both ends**.

## What the dates do to the summer of 1802

| | |
|---|---|
| **12 July 1802** | **ROSA FALCO** marries Carlo Antonio Morgillo. Her father is alive. |
| **18 July 1802** | **MATTEO FALCO dies**, «omnibus Sacramentis munitus» — he had time to receive them — and is buried in the archpriest's cemetery. |
| **4 August 1802** | **GIUSEPPE FALCO** marries Antonia di Guida, **seventeen days after burying his father**. |

Both marriages are on one leaf of the marriage register, and this archive read that leaf this morning
believing the father was alive at both. **He was alive at one of them.**

> A detail, recorded and not built on: **the very next entry in the death register is another death
> of 18 July 1802** — «ALEXANDER […] VIR FORTUNATAE DI SALUZZO, *degens in hac Terra Argenti,
> adscriptus Cohorti Equestri Provinciali hujus Terrae*, aetatis suae annorum 46 circiter, **a
> s[clopeti] bellici igne interfectus, absque Sacramentis Ecclesiae obiit**» — a provincial cavalryman
> **killed by gunfire**, and dying without the sacraments. Something violent happened at Arienzo that
> day. **Matteo Falco's own act says he received all the sacraments, which is not how a man dies
> suddenly**, so the two are probably unconnected. Probably.
