# The parish marriage indexes, read — 109 leaves, and what they hold

**14 September 2026.** The archive has said for months that the alphabetical indexes «*are the proper
instrument for this question, and they are the next job*». That job is now done for everything
reachable from the cached sweeps.

## How they were found

**Not by a query.** Index leaves were detected **structurally** across 2,256 cached transcript pages:
many short name-and-number entries, almost no «*Anno Domini*». That gives **109 index leaves placed at
Arienzo**, of which **32 are couple-indexes** — the marriage kind, entries shaped
«*Groom, et Bride — fol. N*» — and the rest single-name leaves, which are the **death** indexes.

**A trap worth writing down:** a loose *Falco* regex matches **folio** itself. The transcripts render
that word as *fol, folo, folio, fal, fel, felo, fola, Sol, Col, dol, pol*. Requiring the **c**
(`Fal[cgk][oa]`) cuts 315 false hits to 71 real ones.

this archive spent months doing by hand.

## THE FIND: generation six's church marriage

The archive held the **civil** side of Raffaele Falco × Costanza Maione, 1897 — the processetti
dossier. It had no parish act. **The index has them at folio 153**, read from the image off the **R**
leaf `3Q9M-CSMY-B7W5-Q`:

> «Raphael Falco et **Constantia Maione** — 153»

between «*Raphael Rivetti et M.ᵃ Carmela Loffredo — 152*» and «*Raphael De Masco et Maria Iacone —
154*».

**The machine read the bride «Constantia MATORE».** Martone is a real Arienzo surname, and this
archive already holds an **Anna Martone** as Costanza Maione's *own mother* — so the wrong reading was
not merely wrong, it was **corroborable**. The most dangerous OCR error is the one that lands on a
name you already hold. One render settled it.

**The act itself is not yet located.** A sweep of 981 Arienzo pages of `M9J1-9B4` for *Maione* does not
surface the page at folio 153.

## AND NO MATTEO

**No couple-index leaf holds a marriage of a Mattheus Falco.** The only *Matteo Falco* entries in any
index are on the **death** leaves (`B743-Y` and `BQBK-Q`, two filmings of one leaf, where the machine
gives «Marthaus Falco» and «Marisus Falco») — and those almost certainly point at his burial of
**18 July 1802**, which this archive now has from the image.

**Why that is expected rather than disappointing:** the folios tell you the date range. The *Joseph*
leaf puts the 1802 marriages at folios **3** and **6**; the *R* leaf runs to **154**. These indexes
index registers that **begin about 1800**. They were never going to reach the 1760s. The archive's
earlier guess — «*none of those indexes covers the 1760s yet*» — is now a measurement rather than an
impression.

**So the instrument is right and the volume is wrong.** What is needed is the index to an
*eighteenth-century* Arienzo marriage register, or the register itself read directly.
