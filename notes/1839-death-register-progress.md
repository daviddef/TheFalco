## What this volume gave the direct line

### 1. The two Falco stand in the town hall together — atto 102, 26 November

> «…sono comparsi **GIUSEPPE FALCO, di anni CINQUANTATRÉ, di professione COLONO**, regnicolo,
> domiciliato ivi **strada CAMELLARA**, e **VINCENZO FALCO, di anni TRENTASETTE, di professione
> CONTADINO**, regnicolo, domiciliato ivi **strada CAMELLARA**…»

Plate: `site/public/plates/falco-1839-atto102.jpg`

**The first act the archive has with both men in it at once.** Until now they were two separate
sightings three months apart; here they walk in side by side, from the same street, to report the
death of a seventy-year-old neighbour. Whatever else is unproven about generation three, **these two
men knew each other and lived on strada Camellara in November 1839.**

### 2. And the same volume swaps their trades and their ages

| act | month | Giuseppe Falco | Vincenzo Falco |
|---|---|---|---|
| **36** | March | — | **42**, ***colono*** |
| **57** | June | **53**, ***contadino*** | — |
| **81** | September | — | *contadino* (named as Filomena's father) |
| **102** | **November** | **53**, ***colono*** | **37**, ***contadino*** |

**The two trades change places inside a single year, in one register, in one clerk's hand.** And
Vincenzo is **forty-two in March and thirty-seven in November** — five years younger in eight months.

This is the plainest evidence the sweeps have produced for something the archive has always said:
in these registers **an age is not a date and a trade is not an identity.** *Colono* and *contadino*
were both what a clerk wrote for a man who worked another man's land, and he used them
interchangeably for these same two men within the year. **No identification on this site rests on
either.**

### 3. A new child of generation three — atto 81, 13 September — and a conflict

> «…è morta nella propria casa **FILOMENA FALCO d'un anno**, nata in Arienzo, di professione
> contadina, domiciliata ivi, **figlia di VINCENZO FALCO**, di professione contadino, domiciliato
> ivi, **e di ANDRIANA CIOFFI**, domiciliata ivi.»

Plate: `site/public/plates/filomena-falco-1839.jpg`

Read twice, at 3000 and 3400 px, in two separate crops. The mother's surname is **CIOFFI**.

**The archive publishes generation three as Vincenzo Falco × ANDREANA CRISCI**, on the strength of
Raffaele Falco's act. **Both readings now stand and neither is being quietly dropped:**

- **two men called Vincenzo Falco**, one married to a Crisci and one to a Cioffi — Arienzo held
  several Falco households and this is not far-fetched; **or**
- **one man, and one of the two acts is misread.**

The second possibility is live, because **this archive has already been caught by exactly this pair
of shapes**: five years of «Cossi» turned out to be «Cioffi». Settling it needs Raffaele Falco's act
re-read at 3000 px and, if it can be found, the marriage itself.

**What corroborates Cioffi here:** the first declarant is **Marcantonio Cioffi, 26, *calzolajo* of
strada Camellara** — the family's own street, and very likely the mother's kin. He and Domenico
Morgillo had declared together five days earlier at atto 76.

## What it gave the surrounding families

### A second MAIONE household, and a generation behind it — atto 96

> «…è morto nella sua propria casa **FILIPPO MAJONE d'anni SETTANTA**, marito di Maria Martini(?),
> nato in Arienzo, di professione contadino, domiciliato ivi, **figlio di BARTOLOMEO MAJONE
> Defunto**, di professione contadino, domiciliato ivi, **e di FRANCESCA DIGLIO Defunta**.»

Plate: `site/public/plates/maione-1839-a96.jpg`

The register spells it **MAJONE**, with the -j-. Filippo was born about **1769**, which puts his
father **Bartolomeo** in Arienzo around **1740** — the earliest Maione this archive holds. With
**Lucrezia Maione** at atto 88 (wife of Pietro Zimbardo, strada Croce) that is **two Maione
households in one town in one year**. **Neither is joined to Costanza Maione and neither is claimed
to be.**

### The apothecary's own death — atto 95 — and a second conflict

> «…è morto nella sua propria casa **Don GIOVANBATTISTA RIVETTI d'anni CINQUANTACINQUE**, nato in
> Arienzo, di professione **FARMACISTA**, domiciliato ivi, **figlio di GIUSEPPE RIVETTI Defunto**,
> di professione **POSSIDENTE**, domiciliato ivi, **e di LUCIA CIOFFI Defunta**.»

Plate: `site/public/plates/rivetti-farmacista-1839.jpg`

Born about **1784**. The Rivetti page carries **Don Giuseppe Rivetti the elder × Giovanna Bega**,
both dead before March 1819. This act gives Giuseppe a wife called **Lucia**, not Giovanna. Two
Giuseppe Rivetti, a second marriage, or a misreading on one side — **not decided, and both readings
stay up.** Lucia's surname is itself unsafe: *Coppa*, *Cioffi* and *Coppola* are all live at this hand.

**And the man who reported it was VINCENZO MORGILLO, 38, of strada Monticello** — whose wife
**Felicia Rivetti** had died ten months earlier, on 30 January 1839, aged thirty-seven, daughter of
Antonio Rivetti *contadino*. So a man married into the **labouring** Rivetti walks in to report the
death of the **gentry** Rivetti. **That is not proof the two families are one.** Monticello was his
own street and neighbours declared for neighbours throughout these registers. It is the first thing
the sweeps have found that touches both at once, and it is set down as that and nothing more.

### The other Morgillo — atto 110, the volume's last act

> «…è morto nella sua propria casa **Don FRANCESCO MORGILLO d'anni VENTOTTO**, nato in Arienzo, di
> professione **GENTILUOMO**, domiciliato ivi, **figlio di Don LELIO MORGILLO**, di professione
> **DOTTORE**, domiciliato ivi, **e di Donna CARMELA D'AMBROSIO**, domiciliata ivi.»

**The Rivetti pattern again, in the other flanking surname.** In one town in one year this register
buries **Gennaro Morgillo the *vaticale*, Vincenzo Morgillo the *contadino* of Monticello, Nicola
Morgillo the *calzolajo*, Domenico Morgillo the *contadino* of strada Camellara** — and **Don
Francesco Morgillo, gentleman, son of a doctor**, of strada Terra Murata.

**The archive's Morgillo are the day-labourers.** Angela Rosa Morgillo, who married Raffaele Falco,
comes from the Monticello and San Gaetano households. **Nothing joins the two and nothing here
claims it** — it is recorded so that a *Don Morgillo* surfacing in a future search is not mistaken
for kin.

### The Carfora of Arienzo — atti 80 and 107

A whole Carfora kindred on **strada Signorella**: **Giovanni Carfora, 55** (son of **Nicola
Carfora**, dead by 1839 and so born about 1760, and **Santa del Lago(?)**), **Agostino 32**,
**Francesco 38**, **Michele 42**, **Stefano 36**, and **Agata Carfora** married to Gennaro Sepistella.

**This is not Maria Carfora's family and is not offered as one.** The Annecchino line's probable
Carfora is at **Forchia**, sixty years later, in a different province's registers. The two are set
down together only so that nobody later mistakes the Arienzo Carfora for hers.

### The hospital pair, and what their own ages do

**Angelo Diglio, *oste*** — of the comune of **San Felice** — and **Domenico Migliore, *calzolajo***
report the deaths at the **Ospedale di «Ave Gratia Plena»**. They appear three times in this volume
and again in 1840, and **the register gives them a different age almost every time:**

| act | date | Angelo Diglio | Domenico Migliore |
|---|---|---|---|
| **83** | Sep 1839 | **54** | **60** |
| **90** | Oct 1839 | **58** | **60** |
| **103** | Dec 1839 | **50** | **70** |
| 1840, **86** | Dec 1840 | **70** | **55** |

Four readings each, all off the page, none of them a transcription slip on this side. **Angelo Diglio
is fifty-four, fifty-eight, fifty and seventy within sixteen months.** The hospital is written **«Ave
Gratia Plena»** — the same phrase this archive once misread as a nun's name in 1843.

### Domenico Martone of strada Camellara

**Forty-six at atto 98, fifty-two at atto 101 — six days apart.** Same name, same street, six years.
Either Camellara held two Domenico Martone, or the clerk asked twice and got two answers. **Not
resolved**; both figures stand.

> ### Atto 102 — the two Falco stand in the town hall together, and the register swaps their trades
>
> Read at 3400 px, in the clerk's own words:
> «…sono comparsi **GIUSEPPE FALCO, di anni CINQUANTATRÉ, di professione COLONO**, regnicolo,

> **This is the first act the archive has with both men in it at once.** Until now they were two
> sightings three months apart; here they walk in side by side, from the same street, to report the
> same death. Whatever else is unproven about generation three, **these two men knew each other and
> lived on the same street in November 1839.**
>

>

> |---|---|---|---|
> | 36 | March | — | **42**, ***colono*** |
> | 57 | June | **53**, ***contadino*** | — |
> | 81 | September | — | *contadino* (as Filomena's father) |
> | **102** | **November** | **53**, ***colono*** | **37**, ***contadino*** |
>

> interchangeably for the same two men within the year. **No identification on this site rests on
> either.**

> is not mistaken for kin. Same name, same parish, two worlds — exactly as with [Rivetti](/rivetti).
