# THE CIVIL MARRIAGE THAT IS NOT THERE — Arienzo 1843 and 1844 read out

**13 September 2026.** Generation four married in church on **5 March 1844** — «*Sant'Agnese,
Arienzo*», read from the parish act. Under the Two Sicilies every church marriage had a civil
counterpart: the couple appeared before the sindaco, made the *solenne promessa*, and the parish
priest afterwards certified the celebration in the facing **INDICAZIONE** column. That act names both
fathers AND both mothers, with ages, trades and streets. It is the single richest document this
family could still have.

**It is not in the register.**

## What was read

- **Arienzo, Matrimoni 1844 = `an_ua14248`, 32 images — READ END TO END.** Exactly **29 acts**,
  **6 January to 30 December**, closing with a blank form numbered 30 and the Procuratore del Re's
  certificate. **No Falco appears in any of them**, as groom, as bride, or as a parent.
- **Arienzo, Matrimoni 1843 = `an_ua14247`, 38 images — acts 29 to 35 read** (8 October to
  28 December 1843), the window in which a promise for a March 1844 wedding would fall. **No Falco.**
  The volume ends at **act 35**.
- **There is no index.** Checked, not assumed: the last leaves of both volumes carry the closing
  certificate and no *tavola*.

## What that leaves

Three possibilities, none of them yet tested, and **the archive is not choosing between them**:

1. **The act is in the earlier part of 1843** (acts 1–28), if the promise ran long before the wedding.
   Cheapest next test, and the one to do first.
2. **It is at SAN FELICE A CANCELLO**, the bride's comune of birth — she was born there
   24 February 1818. Against this: the priest at Sant'Agnese called her «*mea Parochiana*» in 1844, so
   she was living in Arienzo by then.
3. **There is no civil act.** Possible, and not as strange as it sounds in a town where the church
   register and the civil register plainly did not always speak to each other.

## AND A WARNING ABOUT WHAT THE COMUNE IS CALLED

Every act read in the **1843** volume says «*Civile del comune di **CORPO D'ARIENZO***», while every
act in the **1844** volume says «*comune di **Arienzo***». Both are catalogued by the Archivio di
Stato di Caserta under the single heading "Arienzo".

**This archive does not yet know what that means** — whether Corpo di Arienzo was a distinct comune
with registers of its own, or simply how the clerk of 1843 styled the main body of the town. It is
recorded here because **it would change what a negative result is worth**: a sweep that eliminated
"Arienzo" for a year may have eliminated only one of two jurisdictions. **Do not treat any 1843 nil
as covering both until this is settled.**

## THE BY-PRODUCT, which is worth more than it looks

Reading 36 marriage acts to find one absent Falco produced something else: **five households living
on STRADA CAMELLARA in 1844** — the street that identifies this family across four generations and
does more work than the surname ever does (see `/which-falco`).

| act | who | on Camellara |
|---|---|---|
| 1844/20 | **Marco Morgillo × Pasqualina Villotto [?]** | **both of them** |
| 1844/21 | Saverio Cioffi × **Francesca Sarnone [?]** | the bride |
| 1844/26 | Raffaele Majone × **Nicoletta Diglio** | the bride |
| 1844/29 | Pasquale Loffredo × **Giovanna Margarita Landato [?]** | the bride |

**Marco Morgillo matters most.** Generation four's wife was **Angela Rosa MORGILLO**, and she married
into a Camellara household in March 1844. In October of the same year a **Marco Morgillo of strada
Camellara, 24, contadino, son of the late Pasquale Morgillo**, married a woman of the same street.
**No kinship is claimed.** But a Morgillo household on the Falco street in the Falco year is exactly
the kind of coincidence this archive exists to record rather than to assert.

All 36 acts are in `data/arienzo-civil-marriages-1843-1844.tsv`, with every uncertain word marked
`[?]` and three rows marked **NOT SECURELY READ** rather than guessed.

## CHECKED AGAINST WHAT WAS ALREADY HELD — and one household joined up

The standing rule is that a find is checked against the archive in the **same pass that publishes
it**. Doing that here joined a household across twenty-six years:

- **1844, act 26.** NICOLETTA DIGLIO, 24, contadina, **of strada Camellara**, daughter of the late
  **NICOLA DIGLIO, colono**, marries Raffaele Majone.
- **1818, death register (`an_ua14319`, image 85).** The declarant at a death on strada Camellara is
  **NICOLA DIGLIO, 36, contadino, of strada Camellara** — with **GIUSEPPE DIGLIO, 41, contadino**, of
  the same street beside him.
- **And the death being declared is that of a NICOLETTA DIGLIO**, daughter of Raffaele Diglio and
  Rosa Martone.

So the bride of 1844 **carries the name of a Diglio woman buried on her own street in 1818** — the
same naming-after-the-dead pattern this archive has already documented inside the Falco line, where a
second Gioacchino took the name of a brother buried in 1777, and a second Giovanna took her dead
sister's. **No descent is claimed**: Nicola Diglio the declarant of 1818 and Nicola Diglio the dead
father of 1844 share a name, a street and a trade, which is strong but is not an act.

**Strada Camellara is now documented as a working-class street of Diglio, Morgillo, Sarnone, Landato
and Falco households** — which is exactly what makes it evidence when a record gives nothing else.
