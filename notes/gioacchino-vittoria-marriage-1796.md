## 1. THE FOUNDER WAS ALIVE IN 1796 — and, on the same evidence, in 1802

«**FILIUM MATTHEI, ET FRANCISCAE CRISCI**» — **no *quondam*, before either name.**

**This scribe uses *quondam*.** On the 1802 leaf, in the act that marries Matteo's daughter Rosa, he
writes «Carolum Antonium Morgillo filium **quondam** Caroli Antonii, et Emiliae» — the groom's father
dead, the mother living, and the word placed exactly where it belongs. Six years earlier he writes
Matteo and Francesca with no such word, and in **August 1802** he does it again for their son Giuseppe.

> **So MATTEO FALCO AND FRANCESCA CRISCI WERE BOTH ALIVE ON 4 AUGUST 1802.**

### What that does to the central question

This archive has spent months on Matteo Falco's death. It already knew two things:

- the earliest document naming him, of **1814**, calls his son «Pasquale Falco **del fu** Matteo» — so
  he was dead by then;
- **all six civil death indexes from 1809 to 1814 have been read page for page**, and no Falco of his
  generation is in them.

Add this, and the window closes hard:

> **MATTEO FALCO DIED BETWEEN AUGUST 1802 AND THE OPENING OF THE CIVIL REGISTERS IN 1809 — and his
> death is therefore in the ARIENZO PARISH DEATH REGISTER for those seven years.**

Seven years, one register, one parish. **That is the most precise the question has ever been.**

> **The weight this carries.** It rests on an argument from silence — the absence of *quondam* — and
> arguments from silence are what this archive distrusts most. It is stated here **with the evidence
> for the scribe's own practice attached**, because that is what makes it an argument rather than a
> guess. It is not proof that he was alive; it is strong evidence, and it is labelled as evidence.

## 2. VITTORIA CIMMINO'S PARENTS: MATTEO CIMMINO AND MARIA MIGLIORE

A generation this archive did not have. Note that **both Gioacchino's father and Vittoria's father
were called Matteo** — which is exactly the kind of coincidence that makes a name useless as an
identifier here.

## 3. GIOACCHINO MARRIED AT SEVENTEEN

He was baptised **2 February 1779**. On **16 May 1796** he is **seventeen years and three months old**,
and by **6 May 1797** he is a father.

This was the open question published earlier today, and the act answers the part of it that mattered:
**the 1796 groom is unambiguously «filium Matthei et Franciscae Crisci»**, so the possibility that the
1797 father was some other Gioacchino Falco is **eliminated**.

What remains is that seventeen is young. The alternative — that our man is the **first** Joachim,
baptised 25 November 1774, who would have been twenty-one — **is closed by that entry's own margin**:
a drawn cross and «**obiit die 11 Decembris 1777**», read at magnification, and corroborated by a
«Joachim Falco infans» in the parish death register of 1777.

> **So the correction published this morning stands, and is now better supported than when it was
> made: Gioacchino Falco was SEVENTY-TWO at his death in 1851, not the seventy-six his death act
> states.** A clerk guessing an old man's age would naturally reach for the number that fits a man who
> married at twenty-one. He married at seventeen.
