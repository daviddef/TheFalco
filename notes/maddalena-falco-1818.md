# Maddalena Falco, baptised 7 June 1818 — and the two contradictions it exposed

**Read from the image, 14 September 2026.** Ark `3Q9M-CSD3-1381-R`, Arienzo parish registers,
Sant'Andrea Apostolo. Full image 3742×2758, level 12.

## The act

> «Anno D̄ni **1818**, die vero **septima 7.ᵃ Junij**, R̄ndus D̄nus D. **BLASIUS** Man=|sionarius
> Vicarius Substitutus baptizavit infantem **natam** ex **JOSEPHO FALCO** et **ANTONIA DI GUIDA**
> Conjugibus hujus Parochiae, cui dedit nomen **MAGHDALENA**; Matrina fuit **DORO=|thea d'ADDIO**.»
> — signed **Hyacinthus Archipr. Morgillo**.

Margin: «**Maddalena Falco**».

The celebrant is named by forename and office only — *Don Biagio, mansionario*, acting as substitute
vicar. The act is **filmed twice**: the same entry is on `3Q9M-CSD3-13LG-7`, whose machine transcript
independently renders the godmother «Dorothea il ' Adodo». That is a rare thing in this archive — a
second, independent reading of the same handwriting, and it agrees.

## Finding it cost three renders it should not have

7 June 1818 holds **at least two baptisms at Arienzo**. The first is **Antonius Spazio alias
Sansone**, son of Angelo Spazio alias Sansone and Marta Marrone, godmother Catharina Ruggiero.
Maddalena is the act immediately below it.

**Method note that saved the rest:** read the **margin column alone** first — nine tiles instead of
eighteen. Every act in this register carries the child's name in the margin, so the margin strip
identifies which act is which at a quarter of the rate-limit cost. Only then render the body.

## She was not a new person — and that is the point

The archive **already held her**, twice, from the civil side:

- her **birth act** of the same day, 7 June 1818 — Antenati `an_ua14415` img46r act57, giving the
  father as thirty-six, *contadino*, of **strada Camellara**, the mother as thirty;
- her **death**, **31 May 1825**, atto 30, aged seven, a week short of her seventh birthday,
  declared by Cesare and Francesco Cossi of the same street. Giuseppe and Antonia buried two
  children in nineteen days that spring — Maddalena and Clemente, fifteen months.

So the baptism adds the **church** counterpart of a known day, the **godmother**, and the celebrant.

## Two contradictions, both inside this archive

Checking the find against existing holdings — in the same pass, as the rule requires — turned up
**no new person and two of this site's own errors**:

1. **A stale open question.** `/open-questions` still asked «What became of three daughters?» and
   named Maddalena among them. `/searched` had answered it: she died in 1825. Now narrowed to two
   daughters, with the reason it used to say three left visible.
2. **A false «not established».** `/families` said «*no record yet names Antonia's parents*». Her own
   marriage act of 4 August 1802 calls her «*filiam Josephi e Magdalenae*». The open question is
   whether that Giuseppe is the massaro of strada Maddalena — not whether a record exists.

## And one genuinely open thing

The 1802 act's reading of the mother's surname **splits between two files**: `PIVERA`
(`data/households.tsv`) and `RIVETTA` (`data/arienzo-parish-acts.tsv`, in a row marked *read from
the image*), off the one ark `3Q9M-CSMY-B74R-4`.

**Traced to source the same day, and the split is not even.** The page's machine transcript reads
«*Antonian di Guida filian Josephi e Magdele **PIVERA***» — so `households.tsv` is carrying **the
OCR, copied**. And the OCR fails on this very letter three lines earlier: in Rosa Falco's marriage
of 12 July 1802, on the same page, it renders what are almost certainly Rivetta as «**Riveta**»
once and «**Rivera**» once.

So this is a machine artefact weighed against a human reading, not two witnesses. **Neither form is
adopted** — RIVETTA is also the commonest surname in the corpus, which is precisely the shape of
the «proja» error. The act still wants re-rendering at magnification.

## A thread worth pulling: the d'Addio at the font

**Dorotea d'Addio** stands godmother here in **1818**. A **Dorotea d'Adio** was *commater* at
**Gioacchino Falco's baptism on 2 February 1779** — the founders' own son, thirty-nine years
earlier. One woman of about sixty, or her namesake. The d'Addio are a documented Arienzo family:
a **Mariantonia d'Addio**, wife of Pellegrino Morgillo, is the murder victim whose case is sewn into
the 1827 death register. **No claim is made.** It is recorded because godparent choice is one of the
few things in these registers that tracks real association rather than administrative convenience.
