# Arienzo civil death register 1817 — sweep complete

**Volume:** `an_ua14319` (Arienzo, Morti 1817), 89 images. Read in full, image by image.
**Acts:** roughly 130 in 1817, running 6 January – 28 December, plus **the first two months of 1818**
bound in at the back (images 77–88). The last image is a blank form.
**Officers:** **Daniele Sorice, Sindaco** to about April; then **Andrea Sorice, Sindaco**; with
**Salvatore Scarpati / Frangiato, secondo eletto** deputising through late August and September.
**Forms:** the volume was written on **1816-printed forms** — the clerk strikes the printed «sedici»
and writes **«diciassette»** above it, act after act. Same layout as 1816: no age is given for the
deceased, and the parents and spouse run together low in the act.
**Crop that works:** left `8,19,46,23`, right `50,19,46,23`, width ~980–1200. **This band is wider at
the top than the one used for 1829–36 on purpose** — the tighter band clips the FIRST declarant's
name, and in this volume the first declarant is twice a Falco.
**Duplicate bindings:** 23≡22, 28≡27, 30≡29, 39≡38, 65≡64, 67≡66, 73≡72.

## Why the volume is so big

1816 had 84 death acts across 47 images. 1817 has about 130 across 89. That is not an artefact of
binding: **1817 was the famine year.** Tambora had erupted in 1815, 1816 was the year without a
summer across Europe, and the failed harvest reached southern Italy a year later. The register reads
like it. Whole households go within days of each other —

- **Clemente Giovanni Esposito** died 1 June; his mother **Gaetana Gabriele** on 4 June.
- **Paolo Verdicchio** died 8 June; his mother **Rosa Turco** on 9 June; his son **Raffaele
  Verdicchio** had gone on 8 May.
- **Domenico Turco**, 70, declared his sister's death on 9 June and was dead himself by 30 June;
  his widow **Francesca Sansone** followed on 10 August.
- **Stefano Vallone** buried his daughter **Carmina** on 19 June and was buried himself on 24 June.
- **Sara Ruggiero** died 13 December; her daughter **Fortunata Sossi** on 20 December.

## The find this sweep was for

### The burial of the first Raffaele Falco — image 61 (left), 28 October 1817

> …sono comparsi **SABATO VASSALLO**, di anni **ventisei**, di professione **sportellaro**,
> domiciliato in questo comune, **strada Porta di Sopra**, e di **GIUSEPPE FALCO**, di anni
> **trentasette**, di professione **massaro di campagna**, domiciliato in questo comune,
> **strada Camellara**, ***PARENTI***, i quali hanno dichiarato che a' detto dì **ventotto** del
> mese corrente di **Ottobre** ad ore **sette**, **RAFFAELE FALCO** *figlio di* **VINCENZO FALCO**,
> *e d'* **ANDREANA CRISCI**.

Plate: `site/public/plates/raffaele-falco-death-1817.jpg`. Verified word by word at full resolution.

**This settles generation four.** Vincenzo and Andreana registered a son Raffaele on 25 April 1816
and another on 10 May 1818. The archive could not say which was the ancestor and carried the
generation as *disputed* rather than assert the likelier answer. This is the first one's burial, at
about eighteen months. **The Raffaele of 10 May 1818 is the one who lived** — who married Angela
Rosa Morgillo and died in 1880.

**And it welds the street a second time.** The declarants are «parenti» — kin. Giuseppe Falco is a
documented son of Matteo; Vincenzo is Matteo's grandson through Pasquale. That makes Giuseppe the
dead child's great-uncle, and the register's own word for the relationship is *parente*. It is the
second time these registers use it of a Falco declarant on strada Camellara; the first is the 1831
act (see `notes/1831-vincenzo-parente.md`).

## The other Falco in this volume

> campagna**, domiciliato in questo comune, **strada Maddalena**, e di **ANIELLO DIGLIO**, di anni

> fu* **GIUSEPPE FALCO** *e* **ROSA SOSSI**, ***vedova di FRANCESCO QUOTOLO***.

Plate: `site/public/plates/maria-falco-death-1817.jpg`

**A third founding-generation Falco couple.** Beside Matteo Falco × Francesca Crisci and Michele
Falco × Vincenza Esposito, there was a **Giuseppe Falco × Rosa Sossi**, both already dead by July
1817. This Giuseppe is not any Giuseppe Falco already recorded — the others are alive in 1816 and
1818. And it is a **Falco–Quotolo** marriage, on strada Maddalena.

### Angela «seu Lella» Majone — image 88 (right), 27 February 1818

> …**ANGELA, seu LELLA MAJONE**, *figlia delli fu* **NICOLA MAJONE**, *e* **MARIA FALCO**,
> ***moglie di GIUSEPPE SOSSI***.

Plate: `site/public/plates/angela-majone-death-1818.jpg`

A **second Falco marriage**, into the Majone. Whether this Maria Falco is the one buried seven
months earlier is **deliberately left open**: a woman twice widowed fits the dates, and so do two
women of one name in a town where Falco is common. Nothing in either act decides it, and the archive
records both households separately with the question written into the evidence.

Four acts inside nineteen months, four different implied birth years spanning 1780–1784. The street
is constant and so, three times out of four, is the trade. This archive treats them as one man and
says so, rather than silently averaging the ages.

## Traps in this volume

- **«Giuseppe FALIO», 38, contadino** (image 72 left, 20 December) is **not** a Falco. Enlarged at
  full resolution, the last two letters are «io», not «co». Recorded here so nobody counts him.
- **Two Nicola Morgillo at least.** 28 and of strada Sferdarola on 15 July; 36 and of strada
  Capodiuomo on 21 August; 28 and of strada Sossi on 28 September; and a fourth, 30, a *cavajolo*
  of **strada Camellara** in January 1818.
- **Two Francesco Quotolo.** One is dead before July 1817 (Maria Falco's husband). The other is
  alive and declaring on strada Maddalena — given as 39 on 28 September and 33 on 21 October, three
  weeks apart, which is one man loosely aged rather than two.
- **Two Nicola Majone.** One married Maria Casella (son Giovanni died 9 July 1817); one married
  Maria Falco. Different couples.
- **Two Giuseppe Rivetti?** 25 and a *vaticale* of strada Sant'Andrea in April; 38 and a *bracciale*
  of **strada Camellara** in December. Probably two men.

## What else the volume gave the archive

**A Rivetti population, and some of it on the family's own street.** Chiara Rivetti married Pasquale
Falco, and until now the Rivetti were a surname with almost nothing behind it. This volume names:
**Francesco Rivetti** (35, *vaticale*, strada Sant'Andrea — three sightings), **Salvatore Rivetti**
(44, same trade and street), **Giuseppe Rivetti** (25, Sant'Andrea; and 38, *bracciale*, **strada
Camellara**), **Nunzio Rivetti** (35, *bracciale*, **strada Camellara**),
**Maria Rivetti** (daughter of Paolo Rivetti and Caterina Madonna, wife of Giuseppe Martone),
another **Maria Rivetti** (daughter of Francesco Rivetti and Andreana Laudato), **Alessandra
Rivetti** (wife of Domenico d'Onofrio) and **Michedalena Rivetti** (see below).

**A Morgillo household on strada Camellara, in writing.** This archive has said the Morgillo were
on that street before they were in-laws; image 79 right proves it — **Nicola Morgillo, 30,
*cavajolo*, strada Camellara**, declaring the death of Nicola Morgillo, son of the late Nicola
Morgillo and Rosolina Bruno.

**A gentry Morgillo branch.** **Don Luigi Morgillo**, son of **Don Lelio Morgillo** and **Donna
Carmela d'Ambrosio**, died 20 November 1817. The Morgillo this family married were *bracciali*.
This is the same pattern as the «de Falco» of 1836: one surname, two stations, no join established.

**Three surnames the Falco married into, in one act.** Image 59 right, 21 October: **Angelo Andrea
di Guida**, *figlio di* **Giuseppe di Guida** *e* **Michedalena Rivetti**, *marito di* **Teresa
Vigliotta**. Giuseppe Falco married an Antonia **di Guida**; Pasquale Falco married Chiara
**Rivetti**; Michelangelo Falco married Mariantonia **Vigliotta** and the strada Costa Giuseppe
married Gelsomina Vigliotta. Whether Antonia di Guida is a daughter of this house is a **lead and
not a finding** — nothing here names her.
Plate: `site/public/plates/angelo-andrea-di-guida-death-1817.jpg`

**A Crisci household.** Image 14 left, 13 April: **Faustina Crisci**, daughter of the late **Angelo
Crisci** and **Elena Buono**, wife of Vincenzo Diglio. Her son **Carmine Diglio** followed her on 28
October. Angelo Crisci is a Crisci head this archive did not have. Also **Ruggiero Crisci**, 61, of
strada Porta di Sopra, declaring in January 1818 — and Ruggiero is the surname the parish record
gives Andreana Crisci's mother.

**The clergy and gentry of Arienzo, incidentally.** Padre Agostino Pignatelli; Don Antonio Martone
and Don Biaggio Giglio, both *sacerdoti*; Donn'Angela Piccolo, widow of Don Tomaso d'Ambrosio;
Don Giuseppe Migliore; Don Sabbatino Sossi; Don Gianvincenzo Sanseverino; Suor Andreana Sgambato.

## Two originals exist, and they do not always agree — read the clearer one

`an_ua14320` (Arienzo, Morti 1818) opens with the **same acts** that close this volume: its images
3–13 are the 1818 acts that `an_ua14319` carries at images 78–88. They are **two independent
originals** of the same register — the law required a second copy — written out by two clerks, and
they differ in detail.

The one that caught this archive out: the declarant at the death of Maria Rivetti on 1 January 1818.
In `an_ua14319` this reading first recorded **«Luigi Rivetti, 75, bracciale, strada Annunziata»**.
The `an_ua14320` copy is cleaner and reads, unambiguously at full resolution, **«Nunzio Rivetti, di
anni trentacinque, di professione bracciale»**, of **strada Camellara**. Name, age and street all
wrong in the first reading, and the corrected version is the more interesting one — another Rivetti
on the family's street. Corrected throughout.

Other small divergences in the overlap: Bartolomeo Diglio 30 vs 31; Francesco Martone 48 vs 34 vs
40 in three different acts.

**The rule this gives:** where an act exists in both originals, read both, and prefer the clearer
hand. An age or a street from a single copy is one clerk's word, not the register's.

## A note on how these volumes are bound

The last images of the **1816** volume hold the first act of **1817**; the last images of the
**1817** volume hold the first two months of **1818**. The binder ran the year's blanks on into the
next year rather than waste the paper. So **when a year's acts seem to stop early, read the tail of
the previous volume** — and the 1818 death register (`an_ua14320`) can be started knowing its first
twenty-two acts are already read, here.
