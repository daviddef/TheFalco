# Arienzo civil death register 1816 — sweep complete

**Volume:** `an_ua14318` (Arienzo, Morti 1816), 47 images. Read in full, image by image.
**Acts:** 84, running 6 January – 27 December 1816. Officer throughout: **Daniele Sorice, Sindaco**,
Comune di Arienzo, Provincia di Terra di Lavoro. No district is named in this year's formula —
the *Distretto di Nola* line that appears in the 1829–36 volumes is not yet in use.
**Layout:** unlike the 1829–36 volumes, the 1816 form runs the deceased, **both parents** and the
spouse together in one sentence low in the act, and gives **no age for the deceased** in most acts.
The crop that works is left `10,26,44,15` / right `52,26,44,15` at width 1200.
**Image 46 (right)** is a blank form and **image 47 holds the first act of 1817**, bound on at the end — not, as first read, a re-photograph of this year's act 1.

## The headline: no Falco died in Arienzo in 1816 — but two Falco appear, and a Falco marriage surfaces

> Sorice Sindaco** ed ufiziale dello Stato Civile del Comune di **Arienzo**, Provincia di **Terra di
> Lavoro**, sono comparsi **GIOACCHINO FALCO**, *di anni trentasei*, di professione **massaro di
> campagna**, domiciliato in questo comune, **strada Camellara**, e di **GIUSEPPE FALCO**, *di anni
> trentadue*, di professione **massaro di campagna**, domiciliato ugualmente nel luogo indicato,
> ***parenti del defunto***, i quali hanno dichiarato che a' detto dì Primo del mese corrente di
> Dicembre ad ore undici **CRESCENZO RIVETTI** *figlio del fu* **PASQUALE RIVETTI** *e di*
> **GIOVANNA PERROTTA**, ***marito di ANGELA FALCO***.

Plate: `site/public/plates/crescenzo-rivetti-death-1816.jpg`
Verified at full resolution, word by word — «Gioacchino», «Camellara», «Pasquale Rivetti» and
«Angela Falco» were each enlarged separately before being written down. A first pass at the low-
resolution strip read the father as *Raffaele*; the enlargement shows **Pasquale**. No age is given
for Crescenzo anywhere in the act.

## Why it matters

**1. Gioacchino Falco is a name this archive had never met.** Thirty-six in December 1816, so born
about **1780** — Pasquale's own generation. A *massaro di campagna*, not a *contadino*: a tenant
farmer working a holding, a step above a day-labourer. And he is **on strada Camellara**, the street
this family is centred on. He is not yet joined to anyone. He is recorded as his own household.

**2. A Falco marriage the archive did not have: Angela Falco × Crescenzo Rivetti.** Angela is named
only as the widow — no age, no parents, no children. But she is a Falco who married a Rivetti, and
the direct line's generation two is **Pasquale Falco × Chiara Rivetti**. That is now **two
Falco–Rivetti marriages** in the same small town in the same generation. It is a pattern worth
stating and **not** a proof of anything: Rivetti is common in Arienzo, and nothing here names
Angela's parents or Chiara's siblings. See the note under *Leads* below.

**3. «Parenti del defunto» — kin.** The declarants are not the usual *vicini* (neighbours). The
register says the two Falco are **relatives of the dead man**, which is consistent with Angela
being sister to one or both of them, and consistent with several other things besides. This is the
second time the Arienzo registers have used *parente* of a Falco declarant; the other is Vincenzo
Falco in the 1831 act (see `notes/1831-vincenzo-parente.md`).

**4. A third Giuseppe Falco sighting, and the ages will not reconcile.**
| Sighting | Age | Trade | Street | Implied birth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Births 1816, act 116 (25 Nov) | 36 | massaro di campo | **strada Costa** | c. 1780 |
| **Deaths 1816, act 76 (1 Dec)** | **32** | **massaro di campagna** | **strada Camellara** | **c. 1784** |
| Births 1818, act 57 (7 Jun) | 36 | contadino | **strada Camellara** | c. 1782 |

The 1 December man cannot be the strada Costa Giuseppe, who was 36 six days earlier. He is most
likely the strada Camellara Giuseppe — Matteo's documented son — with the age loose by two years,
which is ordinary in these registers. But the trade differs too, and a third Giuseppe cannot be
excluded. **He has been attached to the Giuseppe Falco & Antonia di Guida household with the
reservation written into the evidence line, not silently merged.**

## The null that matters most

**No burial of Raffaele Falco here** — all 84 acts read. Vincenzo Falco and Andreana Crisci
registered a son **Raffaele on 25 April 1816** and again on **10 May 1818**, and the point of this
sweep was to find the first one's burial between those dates. It is not in this volume.
**It is in the next one:** `an_ua14319`, image 61 left, **28 October 1817**. Generation four is
settled and no longer `disputed`. See `notes/1817-death-register-progress.md`.

No Falco of any kind is recorded as dying in Arienzo in 1816.

## The gap in this log — now closed

**This section used to say that images 3–39 had been read for Falco and no further.** That was true
and it is no longer. The second pass has been made: images 3 to 39, acts 1 to 71, are now rostered
act by act at the foot of this file, with every declarant's age, trade and street.

The Falco answer for that stretch is unchanged and still firm: **none**. What the second pass
recovered is everything else — and it turned out to matter more than the null did. See below.

## Leads, explicitly not findings

**Two Falco–Rivetti marriages in one generation.** Angela Falco married Crescenzo Rivetti, son of the
late Pasquale Rivetti and Giovanna Perrotta. Chiara Rivetti married Pasquale Falco and was, by her
1843 Latin burial entry, *«filia quondam Nicolai et Anna»*. **Different fathers — Pasquale and
Nicola — so Angela's husband and Chiara are not siblings.** They could still be cousins, or nothing
at all. Rivetti is a common Arienzo name and three unrelated Rivetti households are already on
strada Camellara. Nothing here is asserted.


## What the second pass over images 3–39 found

**CIOFFI on strada Camellara, twelve years early.** The correction made in 2026 — that the household
this archive read for five years as *Francesco Cossi and the third Maria Falco* is in fact
**Cioffi** — is confirmed from the other end. The 1816 register, in a wholly different hand, writes
the name plainly and repeatedly: **Agostino Cioffi, 50**, and **Clemente Cioffi, 13**, of strada
Camellara (act 50); **Nicola Cioffi, 40**, of the same street (act 56); **Arcangelo Cioffi, 40**, and
**Andrea Cioffi** (act 63); **Giuseppe Cioffi, 51, *colono***, of strada Cossi (act 69); a
**Raffaele Cioffi**, natural son of Andreana Cimmino (act 55); and a gentry **Don Raffaele Cioffi**
whose two-year-old is entered as a *gentiluomo* (act 59). The family was on the Falco street in 1816
and it was called Cioffi then.

**The Morgillo, pushed back a generation.** Act 23 buries **GAETANA LAUDATO, seventy-eight,
*«vedova di SIMONE MORGILLO»*** — born about 1738 — and it is declared by **ANGELO MORGILLO,
fifty-eight, of strada Trave** and by **Giuseppe Morgillo, her son**. Angelo at fifty-eight in 1816 is
exactly the **Angelo Morgillo, seventy, *colono* of strada Cappuccini** who declares in 1828 and heads
the Monticello household. **So the head of that house is a son of Simone Morgillo and Gaetana
Laudato**, and this archive's separate entry for «Angelo Morgillo fu Simone» is the same man.
Also in this volume: **Marco Morgillo, sixty, *massaro di campagna*** (act 9), **Nicola Morgillo** of
strada Sant'Andrea (act 15) and **Vincenzo Morgillo, thirty-five**, of strada Capodiconca (act 51).

**Donna Francesca Rivetti, in her own act.** Act 12 gives the gentry candidate for Chiara Rivetti's
parents her own entry: fifty years old, *gentildonna*, of contrada Capodiconca, *«figlia del fu Don
Nicola e Donn'Anna Quotolo, moglie di Don Claudio Cimmino»*. It changes nothing about the open
question; it dates and places her.

**Two Troisi, twelve years apart.** Act 28 buries **Don Vincenzo Troisi, sixty, *MEDICO***, and the
declarant is **Il Canonico Don MICHELE TROISI, fifty-two, *sacerdote*** — the same man who dies at
seventy-two in 1826. The ages agree to the year, which is rare enough in these registers to be worth
saying.

**Trades this volume alone supplies:** *bizzoca* (a lay religious woman — Girolama Martinisi,
eighty-six), *miniscalco* (a farrier), *cantiniere*, *cagliere*, *fabbricatore*, *usciere*,
*corriere*, *saponaro*, *bottegajo*, *cantoniere*. And one pair of **aliases**, which no later volume
uses: *Francesco Crispi **alias Pitone*** and *Domenico Crispi alias Pitone*, of strada Cossi.
