## Why this volume was opened first — and what it gave

Chiara Rivetti, who married Pasquale Falco of generation two, died in **1843**; the only record
naming her parents was a Latin parish burial entry calling her *«filia quondam Nicolai et Anna»*.
**The civil act has now been found.**

### Atto 43, 3 September 1843 — the oldest question in the archive, closed

> …sono comparsi **LUCA CIOFFI**, di anni trentatré, di professione **sartore**, domiciliato in
> strada **CAMELLARA**, e **GIOVANNI CIOFFI**, di anni quarantotto, contadino, domiciliato in strada
> **CAMELLARA**, i quali han dichiarato, che nel giorno **TRE** del mese di **Settembre** anno
> corrente, alle ore **venti**, è morta nella sua propria casa **CHIARA RIVETTI VEDOVA DI PASQUALE
> FALCO**, nata in Arienzo, di anni **OTTANTA**, di professione **CONTADINA**, domiciliata ivi,
> **figlia di NICOLA RIVETTI Defunto, di professione CONTADINO**, domiciliato ivi, **e di ANNA DI
> RUGGIO Defunta**, domiciliata ivi.

Plate: `site/public/plates/chiara-rivetti-death-1843.jpg`

**Two couples had answered to «Nicola and Anna».** The gentry **Don Nicola Rivetti × Donn'Anna
Quotolo**, both with acts of their own in 1816 and 1824. And the labouring **Nicola Rivetti,
*contadino*, × Anna di Ruggio**, named in the 1821 death act of their son Carmine. The archive said
the labouring couple was the better fit — Chiara married a *contadino* and lived among day-labourers
— and then refused to claim it, because a preference is not a record.

**The act writes the father's trade out: *contadino*.** The gentry couple is ruled out. And
**CARMINE RIVETTI**, buried in 1821 at fifty-three as a son of that same couple, **was Chiara's
brother**, born about 1768.

She died at eight in the evening in her own house, having outlived Pasquale by nearly nine years,
and two Cioffi of strada Camellara walked to the town hall to say so.

## What the first seventeen acts hold

### Giuseppe Falco is still on strada Camellara in 1843 — atto 14, 9 May

> …sono comparsi **GIUSEPPE FALCO**, di anni **cinquantasei**, di professione **COLONO**, regnicolo,
> domiciliato in strada **Camellara**, e **Giovanni Diglio**, di anni sessantotto, di professione
> contadino, domiciliato in strada Camellara…

Plate: `site/public/plates/giuseppe-falco-1843.jpg`

**And this is what finally settles the age question.** The register gave Giuseppe Falco's age as
**fifty-six in October 1828**. It gives it as **fifty-six again in May 1843** — fifteen years later.
The two cannot both be measurements of the same man, and no man is the same age fifteen years apart.

The full run now reads **37 · 40 · 46 · 46 · 40 · 56 · 56** across 1817–1843. The archive's
standing method note says ages in these registers move by a year a day; this is the proof. Either
the clerks were writing down a guess, or more than one Giuseppe Falco lived on that street — and
after this act the archive leans to the first, because *fifty-six twice, fifteen years apart* is
what a guess looks like, not what two men look like.

### A Falco woman the archive did not have — atto 17, 10 June

Antonio Cioffi, seventeen days old, of **strada Cioffi**, is entered as *«figlio di RAFFAELE
CIOFFI, contadino»* and — read at strip resolution — *«e di DOMENICA FALCO»*.

**This is recorded as a hypothesis, not a finding.** The surname was not enlarged before this file
was written, and the correction made earlier in this project — that five years of *Cossi* were
really *Cioffi* — is exactly what happens when a surname is read at strip resolution and written
down as fact. **It needs a full-resolution reading before it is claimed.**

### And the street is called Cioffi

The 1843 clerk writes the street of that family as **«strada Cioffi»**, not *strada Cossi*. The 1825
clerk wrote *strada Cossi*. It is very likely one street, named for the family that lived on it, and
spelled by ear. The archive now holds three spellings of one place and says so.

## A near miss worth recording

**Atto 19 is not Chiara Rivetti, and it is close enough to be worth saying so.** Chiara's Latin
burial entry reads *«uxor quondam PASCHALIS Falco, filia quondam NICOLAI et Anna, aetatis suae
annorum OCTOGINTA»*. Atto 19 buries **Anna Zimbardo, eighty**, of **strada Camellara**, *«vedova di
PASQUALE Cimmino, figlia di NICOLA Zimbardo e Rosa Cimmino»*. Same street, same age, a Pasquale for
a husband and a Nicola for a father — and the wrong surnames throughout. It is a coincidence, and it
is logged here so that the next reader does not spend an hour on it.

## What the last fourteen images gave

### A Morgillo brother and sister, nine days apart — atti 49 and 54

**Atto 49, 30 September:** *«Francesca Morgillo, moglie di Luca Domino, nata in Arienzo, di anni
cinquantasei, contadina, figlia di **Gaetano Morgillo** defunto, contadino, e di **Maria Cioffi**
defunta.»*

**Atto 54, 8 October:** *«Nicola Morgillo, nato in Arienzo, di anni cinquanta, contadino, figlio di
**Gaetano Morgillo** defunto, contadino, e di **Maria Cioffi** defunta.»*

The same couple, the same clerk, nine days apart. **Francesca (b. ~1787) and Nicola (b. ~1793) are
sister and brother**, and the repetition settles the mother's surname as **Cioffi** — a reading no
single act could have carried, given what *Cossi* cost this archive once already.

This is a **third** Morgillo elder for the town: alongside Angelo Morgillo, *colono*, whose son
Gennaro died on strada Camellara in August (atto 33), there was a **Gaetano Morgillo × Maria
Cioffi** household on strada Capodiconca. **No connection to Angela Rosa Morgillo, who married
Raffaele Falco, is established** — these are Morgillo of the right town and the right generation,
and that is all the record says.

Counting **Maddalena Morgillo**, living, wife of Alessandro di Lucia (atto 60), and **Anna
Morgillo**, dead, mother of Fortunato Crisci (atto 53), this one volume names **four Morgillo women
and three Morgillo men** across at least three unconnected households.

### A Crisci × Morgillo couple in the founding generation — atto 53

*«Fortunato Crisci, di anni cinquantanove, contadino, figlio di **Pasquale Crisci** defunto,
contadino, e di **Anna Morgillo** defunta.»* Born about **1784** — the generation of Matteo Falco
and Francesca Crisci. Both surnames married into the Falco line. **Nothing links this couple to the
Falco.** It is recorded because a reader who finds it later should find it already logged, not
because it means anything yet.

### Two nuns of the Ave Grazia Piena — atti 57 and 62

Two women died *«nel monastero di Ave Grazia Piena»* five weeks apart: **Suor Elena Giulia
Migliore, 80, *conversa*** (a lay sister; atto 57, 11 October), and **Donna Maria Giuseppa
Calabria, 80, *monaca***, daughter of a *proprietario* (atto 62, 6 November). The distinction
between *conversa* and *monaca* is the class line running straight through the cloister wall —
the lay sister's father was probably a tradesman, the choir nun's a property owner.

Both deaths were declared by **the same two laymen of strada Annunziata**, Salvatore Lettieri
(*vaticale*) and Gaetano Sposato (*sartore*). The monastery did not report its own dead; the
neighbours did.

### A hundred-year-old basket maker — atto 65

*«Saverio Zapparella, vedovo di Maria Martone, nato in Arienzo, **di anni cento**, di professione
**sportaro**.»* Born about **1743**, a maker of rush baskets, dead on 9 December 1843.

**The age is the clerk's, not a measurement.** This same volume gives Giuseppe Falco as fifty-six in
May and fifty-five in August, and gives Salvatore Lettieri as fifty-nine in November and fifty-six
five weeks later. A round *cento* is what a very old man's age looks like when nobody has the
paperwork. It is the **highest age in any Arienzo register this archive has read**, and it is
published as a stated age, not as a fact about a lifespan.

### The register was audited in 1849

Image 44 is not an act. It carries a ministerial letter — *Grazia e Giustizia, Napoli, 4 July 1849,
no. 5322* — approving a procedure for stripping blank leaves out of civil-status registers, with the
*Procuratore del Re* of the Tribunale Civile di Terra di Lavoro certifying that **six blank sheets
were removed from this volume**. Worth knowing before anyone concludes that a gap in the foliation
means a lost act: in this volume, six missing leaves are documented as having been blank.
