# MATTEO FALCO'S OWN MARRIAGE — a bounded negative, and a reason to look somewhere else

**13 September 2026.** The single highest-value record this archive can still hope for: a Neapolitan
marriage act names **both fathers**, so Matteo Falco's own marriage would push the line above him for
the first time.

## What was searched, and how

FamilySearch's full-text collection **`M9J1-9B4`** — catalogued as Arienzo marriages, in practice a
**mixed film** whose pages carry deaths as well (the machine labels them «Death, Marriage»). Its text
is the handwriting-recognition transcript of **whole pages**, and the search **ORs** its terms, so a
two-surname query is useless: «Falco Crisci» returns *more* than «Falco» alone.

**The method that worked** was to enumerate rather than search. **Every marriage act carries the word
«denunciationibus»** — the banns. Searching that word returns **515 pages**; **500 were harvested**
and their transcripts filtered locally.

## The result

**The 1760s are densely covered.** Year mentions across those 500 pages:

> 1760:19 · 1761:19 · **1762:36** · 1763:20 · 1764:6 · 1765:17 · 1766:21 · **1767:25** · 1768:19 ·
> 1769:20 · 1770:10

**So the register for the years Matteo must have married is there.** And:

**NOT ONE of those 500 marriage pages carries a legible «Falco» with a year between 1750 and 1779.**

The single apparent hit, `3Q9M-CSMY-B9DN-T`, dissolved on reading: the «alco» is OCR debris in
«*Sabbatino di Nusso et alco*» — the word is *aliis* — and the page's year is **1865**, not 1765.

The earliest Falco **marriage** page in the whole harvest is **1782**.

## What this negative is worth — and what it is not

**It is bounded, not absolute.** Two ways the act could still be in this collection:
- the page's OCR lost the surname — and this machine is bad at it, rendering the family as *Marteus
  Falco*, *Armeni Falco*, *falco*, *Malco*;
- the act sits on one of the **15 pages not harvested**, or on a marriage page where «denunciationibus»
  itself was mis-transcribed.

**Neither is a reason to keep grinding the same film.**

## THE REFRAME, which is the real outcome

**Today this archive learned where a marriage is recorded, and it was not where we were looking.**

Generation four's civil marriage was hunted through **sixty-four consecutive Arienzo acts of 1843 and
1844** and was not there — because **the promise is made in the BRIDE's comune**, and Angela Rosa
Morgillo's was **San Felice a Cancello**. The same rule governs the church: a couple marries in **the
bride's parish**, which is exactly why Raffaele Falco of Sant'Andrea married at **Sant'Agnese** — «*mea
Parochiana*».

**Matteo Falco is certainly at Arienzo**: he stands as a witness at a marriage there in **1759**
(`3Q9M-CSMY-B7QS-P`), before his own. **But FRANCESCA CRISCI need not have been.** If she was not an
Arienzo parishioner, **her marriage is in her own town's register and no amount of reading Arienzo
will find it.**

**The next move is therefore to find HER, not him** — a Crisci baptism of about 1740–45 outside
Arienzo, and the Crisci of the neighbouring comuni: San Felice a Cancello, Santa Maria a Vico,
Arpaia, Forchia, Cervino.
