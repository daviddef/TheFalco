# The thirty follow-ups after the Arienzo 1866–1910 walk: what each came to (15 November 2026)

The full walk of the Arienzo civil registers 1866–1910 left a list of thirty loose ends. This note says what
each came to, **including the ones that came to nothing and why**. A line here is a statement about what was
read and what was not; none is a nil about a name in a volume nobody walked. Where a fact is in a household, the
row carries its own evidence; this note does not repeat it.

How to read the status column: **done** = read from the image and entered or deliberately not entered;
**not established** = looked for, not found, and the search was not a walk of the volume that would hold it;
**blocked** = the register is outside what the Antenati / the FamilySearch collection holds (the volumes stop at
1910 here, or the town is not in the collection); **lead** = a pointer for a future session, not a finding.

## Two methods learned on the way

- **A marriage annex is a birth certificate.** The Allegati (annexes) filed with a marriage hold certified copies of the
  spouses' birth acts. For a bride or groom whose comune of birth is not on Antenati or whose birth register is large,
  the collection's full-text search on the two parents' surnames can land on the annex leaf directly. The machine text is
  a finding aid; the act is read on the image before anything is written.
- **The collection's covers can be mapped without a cookie.** FamilySearch's browse tree for collection 2043630 lists
  every image ark of a comune's volume; each image's 200-pixel thumbnail loads in the page, and a cover or filming slate
  is the dark one. For Santa Maria a Vico MC5R-M2Q (2,156 images) that gave 53 book boundaries from one pass, and a
  contact sheet of the covers named each register: Nascite 1875 starts at image 1281; Matrimoni 1875 at 1371; Morti 1875
  at 1405; a filming slate at 1474–1477; Matrimoni 1876 at 1568; Morti 1876 at 1603.
