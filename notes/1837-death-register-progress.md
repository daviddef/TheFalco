## The find: ORAZIO MORGILLO — atto 95, 11 September

> «…sono comparsi **ORAZIO MORGILLO**, di anni **QUARANTADUE**, di professione **COLONO**, regnicolo,
> domiciliato ivi **strada NUOVA DI SAN GAETANO**, e **SIMONE MORGILLO**, di anni **TRENTA**, di
> professione **COLONO**…»

Plate: `site/public/plates/orazio-morgillo-1837.jpg`

**This is generation four's father-in-law, and it is the first record of him this archive has ever
had.** Angela Rosa Morgillo, born 24 February 1818, married **Raffaele Falco of generation four**;
her father Orazio has been carried on the family tree alone — **no date, no place, no trade.**

**He now has all three.** Alive on 11 September 1837, aged **forty-two**, so born about **1795** —
**twenty-three when Angela Rosa was born**, which fits without strain. A ***COLONO***, the same rank
the register gives Giuseppe, Vincenzo and Matteo Falco.

**And it places the family.** He lives on **strada Nuova di San Gaetano** and declares beside
**Simone Morgillo**, one of the **San Gaetano brothers** the 1828 volume first assembled — Biagio,
Antonio, Simone and Domenico. This archive has always held the **Monticello de' Cappuccini** Morgillo
and the **San Gaetano** Morgillo apart. **This puts the direct line's own Morgillo in-law among the
San Gaetano group.**

**It does not make him their brother.** A shared street and trade is not a stated kinship. But it is
the first address the line's Morgillo has ever had, and it tells the next search exactly where to
look: **the San Gaetano households, and a marriage of about 1817 to Vincenza Pesce.**

## What the completed count says about 1837

The volume was opened partly to ask a question the archive could not otherwise answer: **1837 is the
year cholera reached the Kingdom of Naples. Does the town show it?**

**Reading all 129 acts answers it.** Deaths by month of death, 125 acts with a legible month:

| | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun | **Jul** | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov | Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **deaths** | 9 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 9 | **23** | 14 | 10 | 9 | 10 | 9 |

**The baseline is astonishingly flat — seven to ten deaths a month for nine months of the year — and
then July is TWENTY-THREE.** Two and a half times normal, with August still half again above
baseline. **July, August and September together are 37% of the year.**

**What this archive will and will not say.** The register **names no causes** — not one act in 129
gives a reason for death — so **this cannot be called a cholera outbreak here**, and it is not.
What can be said is measurable and it is this: **the summer of 1837 killed roughly twice as many
people in Arienzo as any other season of that year**, and the spike is sharp, confined to one month,
and unlike anything in 1838 or 1839.

Three things sit alongside it without proving anything:
- **Two Capuchin priests died in the convent eight days apart** in late September and early October,
  and a lay brother had died in January — **three religious in one year from one small house.**
- **Households lost two members within days**: the Belletta on 15 and 20 June — **a widow for five
  days** — and the della Cava, father and son, on 31 August and 3 September.
- **But those pairs are spread across the year, not bunched in July**, which is an argument *against*
  a single acute epidemic explaining everything the volume shows.

**The count is the finding. The diagnosis is not available, and is not offered.**

## The reader's own bias, measured twice

**At 1800 px I twice read a woman as «MARIA CARFORA» — the single name this archive most wants to
find, the probable mother of Filomena Annecchino — and twice she was somebody else.**

- **1838, atto 77** → at 3400 px, **MARIA CARBONE**, widow of a Carfora.
- **1837, atto 27** → at 3400 px, **MARTA CARFORA**, wife of Domenico Cioffi.
  Plate: `site/public/plates/marta-carfora-1837.jpg`

**Two volumes, two different women, and both times the eye supplied the name it was hunting.** That
is no longer an anecdote about handwriting; it is a **measured property of the person reading**. The
defence that worked both times is written into the method: **when a reading is the one you were
hoping for, zoom it before you write it down.**

It is why **atto 33's widow has her surname left deliberately blank.** At 3400 px it will not resolve
between *Diretti*, *di Ruggio* and *Rivetti* — **and two of those are names this archive is actively
hunting.** Recording a blank is cheaper than recording a wish.

## Claims withdrawn

**Antonio Bernardo.** The **1838** notes offered him as *«the useful counter-case — 70, 76, 70, 70
across four acts, three of them agreeing»* and argued from him that the ages were not random noise.
**Atto 52 makes him FIFTY-THREE in June 1837**, eight months before the first of those seventies, on
a different street. Either two men or seventeen years in eight months. **The counter-case is
withdrawn** and the 1838 note corrected in place.

**Internal consistency.** This file first claimed the register was consistent within a volume and
only broke between them. **The hospital pair settles it properly**, appearing **six times** in 1837
(atti 11, 12, 72, 75, 91, 120):

| | atto 11 | atto 12 | atto 72 | atto 75 | atto 91 | atto 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Angelo Diglio** | 70 | 70 | 70 | 70 | 70 | 70 |
| **Domenico Migliore** | 58 | 58 | 58 | **54** | 58 | 58 |

**Eleven of twelve readings agree.** So: **within a volume this clerk is largely reliable; it is
across volumes that the same two men lose and gain twenty years** — Diglio runs 70 / 54 / 58 / 50 /
70 from 1837 to 1840. **That is the sharpest statement the sweeps can make about the ages, and it
took four volumes read end to end to be able to make it.**

Two ages *do* hold across volumes, and both are worth having:
- **Domenico Belletta**, *sartore* of strada Camellara, **56 in August 1837 and 56 in August 1838.**
- **Marcantonio Cioffi**, *calzolajo* of strada Camellara, **25 in December 1837 and 26 in September
  1839** — two years apart, one year older, exactly right. **He is the man who declares the death of
  FILOMENA FALCO** in 1839, the act whose mother reads **Andriana Cioffi** against the archive's
  published Andreana Crisci. A Cioffi of the Falco street whose own age the register gets right twice
  is **a small point in favour of the Cioffi reading, and no more than that.**

## The families around the line

**MAIONE — and one of them lived on strada Camellara.** Atto 66: **ARCANGELO MAJONE, 28, contadino,
strada CAMELLARA**, beside Nicola Morgillo the shoemaker. Generation six married **Costanza Maione**
in 1876 — **nothing joins her to him** — but the Maione were **neighbours**, not a family in another
quarter. The volume also gives **Nicola, Angelo, Raffaele, Saverio, Domenica, Angela, Elisabetta and
Rosa Majone**, and:

> **atto 41: GIUSEPPE MAJONE, 73, «figlio di BARTOLOMEO MAJONE, defunto»** — plate:
> `site/public/plates/giuseppe-majone-1837.jpg`

The **1839** volume buries **FILIPPO MAJONE, 70, also «figlio di BARTOLOMEO MAJONE Defunto»**. **Both
mothers are a Francesca and the surnames differ** — *Diglio* for Filippo, *Capora/Carfora(?)* here —
so **two Bartolomeo, a second marriage, or a misreading**, and the reading here is not good enough to
choose. **If they are brothers, Bartolomeo was born about 1735 and is the earliest Maione this
archive can name by two generations.**

**And SAVERIO MAJONE and ANTONIA ALTAVILLA bury a son ARCANGELO aged one in August 1837, and a second
son ARCANGELO aged two days in September 1838.** A dead child's name given again — **a fourth
documented case**, after the two Gaetana Cioffi, the two Antonio Zimbardo and the two Andreana
Cimmino. **It is exactly the pattern that merges two children into one life in any index.**

**MORGILLO.** Beyond Orazio: **three Morgillo fathers of the 1730s** now stand behind this volume —
**Simone** (father of the 78-year-old Antonia, atto 64), **Gennaro** and **Sabato** from 1838, and
**Matteo Morgillo** at atto 32. **Three men called Simone Morgillo across three generations** are
kept apart. And **Biagio Morgillo is forty on 1 March and forty-six on 24 July.**

**RIVETTI — a sixth and seventh household.** **BENEDETTO RIVETTI** (atto 88) and **FRANCESCO RIVETTI,
57, «figlio di GENNARO RIVETTI Defunto»** (atto 125). **Gennaro Rivetti is an exact contemporary of
Nicola Rivetti, Chiara's father** — both dead by the 1830s, both fathering in the 1780s, one a
*vaticale* and one a *contadino*. **Nothing joins them.** Atto 105 also completes a household the
archive had only half of: **Salvatore Rivetti**, whose death act is the one where the register calls
the declarant «*NIPOTE*», **now has a wife (Anna Bernardo) and a daughter (Angela Maria, 18).**

**CARFORA on three streets** — Signorella, Santa Lucia and **Camellara** (atto 101, Gennaro Carfora,
19). Seven Carfora across three volumes. **Still nothing joining any of them to Forchia.**

**DI GUIDA — the family that married Giuseppe Falco in 1825.** **Giuseppe di Guida, 91, *colono*,
son of GENNARO DI GUIDA** (atto 33) — three generations, all *coloni* of strada Maddalena — plus
**Clemente di Guida, *sensale***, and **LUIGI DI GUIDA, *AGRIMENSORE***, a land surveyor. **A family
running from day-labourer to land-measurer in one town in one year.**

## The town in 1837

**GIOVANNA PERROTTA, «d'anni CENTO TRE»** — atto 19, born about **1734**, a *contadina*, **the oldest
person the Arienzo sweeps have found**, and born in the same decade as Matteo Falco.
Plate: `site/public/plates/giovanna-perrotta-1837.jpg`. **A Perrotta married to a Perrotta**, like the
Falco–Falco of 1838 and the Carfora–Carfora of atto 27 — **all three undecided between a real
marriage within a surname and a clerk carrying the husband's name across.**

**The Capuchin house, proved.** The archive has known the street name *Monticello de' Cappuccini* for
years. This volume puts the **convent itself** in the register with three of its men: **Fra' Domenico
Dorino, a *laico* of 39** (atto 4); **il Padre di Montesarchio, «nel secolo chiamato Michelangelo
Nanni(?)»** (atto 99); and **il Padre Molto Reverendo LETTORE Raffaele della Torre, «nel secolo
chiamato Salvatore Rizzo(?)», 50, nato in Melito(?)** (atto 102) — **the house's teacher.** Both
priests were incomers, and **both were declared by the same two neighbours, Vincenzo Morgillo and
Raffaele Majone of strada Cappuccini.**

**The registrars were the landowners.** **Don RAFFAELE TROISI, *possidente*** (atto 56) shares the
surname of **Filippo Troisi**, who signs atti 1–18. **Donn'ANNA CILLO, 73, *gentildonna*** (atto 82)
and **Donna ROSA CILLO, 47** (1838, atto 13) are **both «figlia di ALESSANDRO CILLO Defunto,
possidente»** — **sisters joined only by reading two volumes consecutively** — and share the surname
of **NICOLA CILLO**, who signs the rest of 1837.

**Three generations of masons:** **Lorenzo → Domenico → Clemente di Lucia**, the trade named in every
act, **and the last of them was twenty-two** (atti 104, 111). **A father buried three weeks after his
son.**

**And the trades this volume alone records:** *cappellaio*, *maccaronaro*, *lavatrice*, *saponaro* on
**strada Torricella de' Saponari**, *agrimensore*, *sacerdote*, *laico cappuccino*, *barbiere*,
*pettinatore*, *sensale*, *corriere*, *fruttajolo*, *bottegajo*, *vinivendolo*, *rivendugliolo*,
*falegname*, *muratore*, *ferraro*, *sartore*, *calzolajo*, *oste*, *filatrice*, *colono*, *vaticale*.
**These registers are the closest thing this archive has to a trade census of the town.**

**Officers: FILIPPO TROISI, *secondo eletto*, atti 1–18; NICOLA CILLO, *sindaco*, from atto 19.**
