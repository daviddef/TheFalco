# Arienzo civil death register 1820 — sweep complete

**Volume:** `an_ua14322` (Arienzo, Morti 1820), 43 images. **Read in full.**
**Acts:** 66, running 8 January – 30 December 1820. Images 42(right)–43 are blank.
**Officers:** **Andrea Sorice, Sindaco** to about March, then **VINCENZO MORGILLO, Sindaco** —
*a Morgillo is mayor of Arienzo* — with **Angelo Martone, secondo eletto** deputising from September.
**District:** now given as **Distretto di Nola**, printed on the form.

## The form changes, and it changes everything

From 1820 the printed act is far fuller than the 1816–19 one. It gives, for every death:

- **the deceased's age**, birthplace, trade and street;
- **both parents by name**, with their trades and their addresses, and whether each is *defunto*;
- the spouse, where there is one;
- and **each declarant's relationship to the dead person** — *vicino*, *parente*, *fratello*,
  *cugino*, *genero*, *avo*, *zio*, spelled out rather than left to inference.

That is why this single volume yields more about this family than the four before it together.

## ANNA FALCO — atto 47, 23 August 1820

> …sono comparsi **GIUSEPPE MARTONE**, di anni **quaranta**, *Contadino*, domiciliato in questo
> comune, **strada Camellara**, *vicino del defunto*, e **DOMENICO MARTONE**, di anni **trentasei**,
> *Contadino*, domiciliato ivi, strada Camellara … **è morta nella sua propria casa ANNA FALCO
> di anni VENTICINQUE, moglie di ARCANGELO CRISCI, nata in Arienzo, di professione Contadina,
> domiciliata ivi strada Camellara, FIGLIA DI PASQUALE FALCO, di professione Contadino,
> domiciliato ivi strada Camellara, E DI CHIARA RIVETTI, domiciliata ivi.**

Plate: `site/public/plates/anna-falco-death-1820.jpg`

**Anna Falco is a daughter of the direct line's generation two.** Pasquale Falco and Chiara Rivetti
are the couple at the head of this pedigree's second generation; Anna is their daughter and
**Vincenzo Falco's sister**. Born about 1795. The archive did not have her as their child.

**And both parents are named without «fu».** So on 23 August 1820 **Pasquale Falco was alive, a
contadino, living on strada Camellara** — the only civil sighting of him anywhere between his
marriage and his death act of 1 October 1834. **Chiara Rivetti was alive beside him**, and until
this act the only record that named her at all was her own Latin burial entry of 1843.

### Her household, seven months earlier — atto 5, 24 January 1820

> …**è morto nella sua propria casa FORTUNATO CRISCI di giorni due**, nato in Arienzo, domiciliato
> ivi **strada Camellara**, **figlio di ARCANGELO CRISCI**, *Contadino*, domiciliato ivi strada
> Camellara, **e di ANNA FALCO**, domiciliata ivi. — Declared by **ANDREA CRISCI**, 60, and
> **MARCELLO CRISCI**, 25, both *contadini* of strada Camellara.

Plate: `site/public/plates/fortunato-crisci-death-1820.jpg`

Anna buried a two-day-old son in January and was dead herself by August, at twenty-five. Her
husband's household stood on the same street as her father's.

## What this closes

**«Arcangelo Crisci, twice» is fully answered.** The 1817 birth register had shown two men of the
name, aged fifty-two and thirty. This volume says who Anna Falco was: **the line's own daughter**.
The younger Arcangelo, born about 1787, married her; the elder, born about 1765, is Andreana
Crisci's father. So the Crisci married into this family **twice in one generation** — Vincenzo
Falco married Andreana Crisci in 1814, and his sister Anna married Arcangelo Crisci.

**Correction.** This archive previously recorded Anna Falco as dying **«at 22»**. The act says
**twenty-five**.

**«Angela Falco's death of 1829» is probably solved.** The archive relied on a parish entry, read
through machine transcription, in which Pasquale Falco is named without *quondam* on the death of a
daughter **Angela** in **1829** — but the 1829 civil register, read in full, holds no Falco at all.
This act has exactly that shape: a daughter **ANNA**, father Pasquale, no *fu*. *Anna* against
*Angela*, *1820* against *1829* — both are ordinary transcription slips. Recorded as probable and
not proven; the parish images would settle it.

## The rest of the year: no other Falco

Sixty-six acts, and the Falco appear only in those two. But the street does its usual work — the
declarants on strada Camellara in 1820 are **Giuseppe and Domenico Martone**, **Francesco Carbone**,
**Cesare and Alfonzo Giglio**, **Angelo Scafora**, **Tomaso Esposito**, **Bartolomeo Diglio**,
**Giuseppe Crisci**, **Gaetano Sossi**, **Cosimo Rivetti** and **Sabbatantonio Corpora**.

**Other things worth having:**

- **A Morgillo becomes Sindaco.** Vincenzo Morgillo replaces Andrea Sorice in the spring. He is
  very likely the **Don Vincenzo Morgillo** whose daughter Donna Nicoletta died in February 1819.
- **Don Salvatore Scarpati, notary**, died 20 February aged forty-nine — the *secondo eletto* whose
  name is on so many acts of 1817–19.
- **Gentry on strada Camellara.** Donna Angela Raffaella de Angelis, two years old, *gentildonna*,
  died there in November, her cousin **Don Francesco di Angelis**, *possidente*, declaring — also of
  strada Camellara. The family's street was not only labourers.
- **The di Guida of strada Maddalena** appear twice more: **Giuseppe di Guida**, sixty-eight (the
  same age he gives in 1819), and **Raffaele di Guida**, forty to forty-two, *massaro di campagna*,
  once declaring as a *genero*.
- **Don Giovanbattista Rivetti**, the apothecary, loses his wife: **Donna Caterina Espositella**,
  thirty-two, *gentildonna*, died 16 February.
- **The Ospedale di Ave Grazia Plena** at Arienzo appears repeatedly as a place of death, always
  declared by the same pair — Angelo Giglio and Domenico Migliore, both of San Felice.
