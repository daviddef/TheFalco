## A third act for Francesca Falco — atto 50, 17 August

> …è morta nella sua propria casa **MADDALENA CIOFFI d'un anno**, nata in Arienzo, di professione
> contadina, domiciliata ivi, **figlia di RAFFAELE CIOFFI**, di professione contadino, domiciliato
> ivi, **e di FRANCESCA FALCO**, domiciliata ivi.

Plate: `site/public/plates/francesca-falco-1840.jpg`

**The household is now complete across four volumes.** Raffaele Cioffi and Francesca Falco, of
strada Cioffi, buried three children in four years:

| child | age | died |
|---|---|---|
| **Maddalena Cioffi** | one year | **17 August 1840** |
| **Luigi Cioffi** | one day | **2 January 1842** |
| **Antonio Cioffi** | seventeen days | **10 June 1843** |

Both parents are named **without «fu»** in all three acts, so both were alive throughout. **Three
acts, three clerks, one spelling of her name.** The 1843 file published *«Domenica Falco»* as an
explicitly flagged hypothesis; it is now corrected by **two independent readings**, not one. Nothing
here names Francesca Falco's own parents, and the archive still does not guess at them.

## The household of the man who reported Chiara Rivetti's death — atto 73

**Giovanni Cioffi**, *contadino* of **strada Camellara**, stands beside Luca Cioffi at Chiara
Rivetti's act in September 1843. This volume gives him a household of his own: on a November day in
1840 he and **MARIA PORRINO** buried **Marta Cioffi, one month old**. His stated age runs **40**
(1840), **47** (August 1842), **48** (September 1843) — after the first entry, the steadiest series
in these registers. **Virgilio Saccavino**, another Camellara declarant, holds too: **50** here,
**53** in 1843.

## The archpriest's family, and a second religious house — atto 28, 9 April

> …è morta **nel CONSERVATORIO DI SAN FILIPPO [NERI]** — **MARIA GIUSEPPA SCARPATI d'anni
> SETTANTADUE**, nata in Arienzo, **di professione SORORA DEL CONSERVATORIO**, **figlia di Don
> STEFANO SCARPATI defunto**, di professione **POSSIDENTE**, **e di Donna SERAFINA CIMMINO defunta**.

Plate: `site/public/plates/scarpati-1840.jpg`

**Arienzo had a second religious house.** Everything the archive had found until now happened at the
*Ave Gratia Plena*; this is a **conservatorio**, and atto 46 of the same volume confirms the
dedication from the other direction by naming a **strada San Filippo Neri**.

**And she is almost certainly the archpriest's sister.** The 1842 volume buries **Don ANGELO
SCARPATI, 76, *arciprete* of Arienzo**, *«figlio del Signor **Stefano Scarpati** defunto,
**possidente**, e della Signora **Faustina Cimmino**»*. Same father's name, same trade, same rank;
mother's surname **Cimmino** in both; the children born about 1766 and 1768. **The one discrepancy
is the mother's forename** — *Serafina* here, *Faustina* there — which is exactly what a clerk gets
wrong. Recorded as a **strong inference with the discrepancy named**, not as a proven sibling pair.
What it establishes outright is that the archpriest came from a propertied house that put a daughter
into a conservatorio and a son into the parish.

## Deaths away from home, and the strangers

This volume is unusually rich in people who did not die in a bed of their own.

- **Atto 32: a man dead in the road.** *«è morto **nella strada della Porta di Sopra di questo
  comune** — Donato Schettini, d'anni ventisei, ***corriere***»*, born out of town. Two other
  couriers reported it. **The first death in the open street in any register this archive has read.**
- **Atto 12: Don Giuseppe Salvato, 54, *possidente*, born outside Arienzo — both parent lines left
  BLANK.** The third stranger in these registers whose parents nobody could name, and the first who
  was a propertied man.
- **Atto 35: Don Francesco Saverio Lubri, *proprietario*, «nato in NAPOLI»** — and *his* parents are
  named, including a father who was **«GIUDICE DI VICARIA»**, a judge of the high court of Naples.
  **The most senior office named in any act this archive has read.**
- **Atto 83: Giuseppe Zito, 57, «*confettiere*», «nato in ACERRA».**
- **Atto 60: la Signora Giovanna di Mayo, 84, «vedova di Don BARTOLOMEO DE CAPUA»** — a name of the
  Neapolitan nobility.
- **Four more deaths in the Ospedale d'Ave Gratia Plena**, all four reported by the same fixed pair,
  **Angelo Diglio of San Felice a Cancello and Domenico Migliore** — now attested in 1840, 1841 and
  1842.

## One household this volume takes apart

**Pasquale Cioffi**, a *calzolajo* of strada Croce, lost **Filomena, thirteen months, on 11
January**; **Gaetano, two days old, on 1 February**; and on **3 May his wife FRANCESCA CIOFFI, at
thirty-five**. In September he is on his feet again, declaring a neighbour's death (atto 54). The
register does not comment, and neither does this file.

## The trap this volume sets, stated plainly

Names repeat, and the ages do not help. In eighty-seven acts there are:

- **at least two, probably three, men called SEBASTIANO LETTIERI** on strada Costa — with wives
  Felicia Cioffi (August), Margarita Porrino (November), and Mariangela Domino at his death in 1841;
- **two Gennaro Lettieri**, with different wives;
- **two Clemente Zimbardo**, a week apart, with different wives;
- **two Pasquale Morgillo**, a generation apart — one dead by 1840, one fathering a ten-year-old in 1841;
- **a third Gennaro Morgillo** and **a second Marco Morgillo**, both known only as dead husbands;
- **Angelo Cioffi the *sensale*** at 54 in October and 44 in December;
- **Clemente Mauro the *corriere*** at 34 in May, 44 in October, 34 in November.

**Two acts in this volume flatly contradict each other**: atto 22 and atto 25 give **Pasquale Giglio
and Rosa Lettieri** two daughters dying eleven days and twenty-two days old, on 29 May and 8 April
— births eight weeks apart, which is impossible. The register also runs out of date order there.
**Both are published as read, with the contradiction stated.**

## The Morgillo of 1840

**Antonio Morgillo**, *calzolajo* of strada Costa (44 in February, 43 five days later — and 43 again
in 1843). **Caterina Morgillo, 60**, daughter of a **Pasquale Morgillo** dead by 1840. **Domenico
Morgillo, 34**, of **strada Rejolana** — a candidate for the San Gaetano brother of 1828, on the
right street, and not identified. **Andrea and Gennaro Morgillo declaring together on strada Porta
di Sopra**, the street where Carlantonio Morgillo dies in 1842. And **Anna Finelli, 75, «vedova
d'ANGELO MORGILLO»** — possibly the widow of the *colono* of Monticello de' Cappuccini who was
seventy in 1828, **and possibly not**, because a second Angelo Morgillo was alive that year too.
