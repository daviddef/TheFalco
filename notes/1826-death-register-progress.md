# Arienzo civil death register 1826 — sweep complete

**Volume:** `an_ua14328` (Arienzo, Morti 1826), 52 images. **Read in full, act by act.**
**Acts:** 88, running 2 January – 30 December 1826, plus two acts transcribed in from elsewhere.
**Officer:** **SAVERIO CIOFFARI, sindaco**, throughout — he has replaced Giovanbattista de' Napoli,
who signed 1825 as *secondo eletto*.
**Duplicate bindings:** 19≡18, 39≡38, 49≡48. **Blank leaves:** 14L, 50R, 51L.
**At the back:** the same printed **ministerial circular of 4 July 1849** on the removal of blank
leaves that closes the 1825 volume.

## Maria Falco, thirty-two — atto 22, 25 April 1826

> …**è morta nella sua propria casa MARIA FALCO d'anni TRENTADUE, moglie di ALFONSO DIGLIO**, nata
> in Arienzo, di professione Contadina, domiciliata ivi, **figlia di PASQUALE FALCO**, di professione
> **Contadino**, domiciliato ivi, **e di CHIARA RIVETTI**, domiciliata ivi.

Plate: `site/public/plates/maria-falco-death-1826.jpg`

**A second daughter of generation two.** Pasquale Falco × Chiara Rivetti are named as her parents —
which makes Maria a sister of **Vincenzo Falco** and of **Anna Falco**, who died on the same street
in August 1820. Born about **1794**, she had married **Alfonso Diglio**.

**Neither parent carries «fu».** So Pasquale Falco was still alive and still a *contadino* in April
1826, and Chiara Rivetti with him. This is the **second** civil sighting of Pasquale between his
1780s marriage and his death on 1 October 1834 — the first being Anna's act of 1820. The gap in his
life is now 1820 · 1826 · 1834.

The declarants — **Angelo Sansone, 45, contadino** and **Tomaso Sposito, 50, contadino**, both of
**strada Camellara** — declare as *vicini*, not *parenti*. The same two men had buried a five-month-old
di Majo on the same street three days earlier (atto 20).

**Caution on the date.** At strip resolution the day read *ventinove*; the enlargement gives
**venticinque**, and the month, which first read as *Agosto*, is **Aprile** — settled not by the
handwriting but by the act numbers, since atto 23 is dated 4 May. Where a month word in this volume
is ambiguous, the running act number decides it.

## Giuseppe Falco declares twice — and once beside a Morgillo

**GIUSEPPE FALCO, forty-six, *massaro*, of strada Camellara** appears twice as a declarant:

- **atto 56, 9 August** — for **Giuseppe Cossi, two years old**, son of **Gaspare Cossi** and Maria
  Sposito, of strada Camellara. The other declarant is Salvatore Sposito, 26, the child's *zio*.
- **atto 73, 28 October** — for **Sabata Florino, fourteen months**, daughter of Biagio Florino and
  Anna Cossi. His fellow declarant is **CARLANTONIO MORGILLO, forty-six, contadino, of strada
  Camellara**.

Plate: `site/public/plates/falco-morgillo-declare-1826.jpg`

**This is the first act in the whole Arienzo series in which a Falco and a Morgillo stand together.**
Two men of the same age, on the same street, signing the same declaration. It is not a kinship
statement — both are entered as *vicini* — but it is the earliest evidence that the two houses the
line would later join, when **Raffaele Falco married Angela Rosa Morgillo**, were already neighbours
in 1826.

**The age is consistent.** Giuseppe Falco was *massaro di campagna*, 37, of strada Camellara in
October 1817; he is 46 here. Born about 1780. A documented son of Matteo Falco and Francesca Crisci.
Both 1826 readings were checked at full resolution before being written down.

Note that this Carlantonio Morgillo, 46 and of Camellara, is **not** the Carlantonio Morgillo, 30, of
strada Capodichina who declares atto 48 in July. There are now at least three men of that name in
Arienzo; see `1821-death-register-progress.md` and `1825-death-register-progress.md`.

## The Morgillo the register buries

1826 is the richest Morgillo year so far. Five Morgillo appear as principals:

- **Grazia Morgillo, 85**, wife of Giacinto Migliore, daughter of fu Nicola Morgillo and fu Orsola
  Diglio — atto 30, 20 May. Born about 1741.
- **Ovidia Morgillo, 90**, widow of Raffaele Diglio, daughter of fu Gennaro Morgillo and fu Maddalena
  d'Ambrosio — atto 41, 14 June. She died **in the house of her son Bartolomeo Diglio**, and it is
  her other son, **Francesco Diglio, 44, of strada Camellara**, who declares.
- **Angelo Morgillo, 58**, husband of Marianna di Silvio, son of fu **Amore** Morgillo and fu Caterina
  Laudato, of strada Nuova di San Gaetano — atto 68, 4 October. His son **Biagio Morgillo, 30**,
  declares.
- **Margarita Morgillo**, wife of the *possidente* Andrea Fornella (atto 37, 9 June).
- **Lucia Laudato, 70, widow of Mattia Morgillo** (atto 77, 10 November).

And two more as parents: **Saveria Morgillo** (atto 65) and **Rosa Morgillo**, wife of Antonio
Rivetti, of **Monticello de' Cappuccini** (atto 59) — whose fourteen-month-old **Clemenzia Rivetti**
is declared by **Nicola Morgillo, 40** and **Marcello Rivetti, 46**, both of that hamlet. The Rivetti
and the Morgillo of Monticello keep standing for each other.

## Houses, trades and institutions

The 1826 register is unusually full of people who are not *contadini*:

- **Fra' Bernardo de' Angeli, 79, *laico cappuccino*, born in Naples** — died in the **Monastero de'
  Cappuccini** (atto 4). The third Capuchin death recorded across these volumes.
- **Suor Mariangela Rossi, 76, *monaca*** — died in the **Conservatorio di San Filippo Neri** (atto 35).
- **Donna Dorotea Zoppoli, 94, *monaca*** — died in the **Monastero di Ave Gratia Plena** (atto 78).
- **Don Michele Troisi, 72, *sacerdote*** (atto 7), and **Don Giuseppe Crisci (or Cossi — see below),
  56, *sacerdote*** (atto 84).
- **Sig. Saverio Salvatore, 32, *brigadiere*** of gendarmes, born at Nocera de' Pagani — died in the
  **barracks in the suppressed Monastero di Sant'Agostino** (atto 33). Two gendarmes declare it; a
  third, **Nicola Sasseno**, buries a two-month-old son in September (atto 66), declared by **Luigi
  Stefanica, 49, *custode delle prigioni***.
- **Don Raffaele d'Ambrosio, 74, *Regio Notaro*** (atto 72) — in whose house the eighty-year-old
  servant **Teresa d'Addio**, born at Santa Maria a Vico, had died in May (atto 27). Two more of that
  house follow: **Don Marcantonio d'Ambrosio, 30, *amanuense***, son of **Don Fabrizio d'Ambrosio,
  *dottor legale*** (atto 60), and a sixteen-day-old son of **Don Giovanni d'Ambrosio, *dottor
  fisico*** (atto 81).
- **Don Filippo Troisi, *farmacista***, buries a sixteen-month-old (atto 64).
- **Giuseppe di Silvio** died on **1 May in the Ospedale degl'Incurabili at Naples**; his death was
  transcribed into the Arienzo register in October (13R), and eight days after it his six-year-old
  son **Antonio** was buried at home (atto 24), the act naming him *«figlio di Giuseppe di Silvio
  Defunto»*. The two records confirm each other.
- **Donna Andreana Sansone** died at **Recale** on 20 October; transcribed in on 5 November (51R).

## The reading that will not settle

**Atto 84** buries a priest, **Don Giuseppe C-r-i-s-c-i / C-o-s-s-i, 56**, son of **Andrea**, a
*possidente*, and **Orsola Zimbardo**. In this scribe's hand the two surnames are written with the
same figure and cannot be separated at any magnification the images allow. Both families are
present in the volume as gentry — a **Don Raffaele Cossi**, *possidente*, and a **Don Antonio Cossi**,
*possidente*, both declare. It is left unresolved rather than guessed. The same doubt attaches to
**atto 14**, whose deceased is a woman of that surname, daughter of **Virgilio** and **Teresa**, her
own forename struck through by the ruling-out stroke.
