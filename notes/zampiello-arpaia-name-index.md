# THE ZAMPIELLO OF ARPAIA, NAME-INDEXED — 259 records, and a mother for Angelo

**13 September 2026.** Antenati's **name** index (`/search-nominative/`) had never been run on this
family. It gives the person **and their parents**, which the register search does not.

**259 Zampiello records at ARPAIA**, spanning **1862 to 1940** — **222 births, 29 deaths,
8 marriages**. Harvested with `tools/antenati_names.py` into
`data/antenati-names-zampiello-arpaia.tsv` and published in the register, every row marked
**«INDEX ENTRY — the act itself is not read»**.

## The find: TOMMASO ZAMPIELLO × ROSA CRISCI

The archive's Angelo Zampiello is known from the family's own papers as **«Zampiello Angelo **di
Tommaso**»** — the war-cross certificate for East Africa — born **31 August 1895 at Arpaia**, a birth
act this archive has never been able to reach.

The index now gives an Arpaia household in exactly the right place at exactly the right time:

> **TOMMASO ZAMPIELLO × ROSA CRISCI** — son **FRANCESCO MARIA ZAMPIELLO**, born **29 January 1892 at
> Arpaia** (`an_ud1201809`).

**If this is the same Tommaso — and «di Tommaso» plus Arpaia plus the 1890s makes it likely — then
ANGELO'S MOTHER WAS ROSA CRISCI, and Francesco Maria was his elder brother.** Neither is claimed.
**One index entry is not a household**, and Tommaso is not a rare name here: the index holds a Tommaso
born 1871, another born 1875 (son of **Raffaele Zampiello and Giovanna Pecchillo**), and acts of 1892
and 1898 under the name.

## AND A CONTRAST THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE FIND

There is **no Angelo Zampiello born 1895** in the index. That looks like the same problem as
**Filomena Annecchino's missing 1897 birth at Forchia** — and it is not, and the difference is the
point.

**Births indexed per year at Arpaia, 1885–1905:**

> 1885:4 · 1886:2 · 1887:4 · 1888:2 · 1889:4 · 1890:1 · 1891:0 · 1892:3 · 1893:2 · **1894:0** ·
> **1895:0** · 1896:1 · 1897:0 · 1898:2 · 1899:1 · 1900:1 · 1901:0 · 1902:0 · 1903:0 · 1904:1 · 1905:0

**The whole index is thin here — never more than four in a year, and zero in six of them.** Angelo's
absence explains itself: **the source barely covers Arpaia at all.**

**Forchia is the opposite.** Carmine Annecchino and Maria Carfora's children are densely indexed —
Giacinta 1883, Agata 1884, Giacomo 1887, Agata 1889, Lucia and Alfonso 1892, Alfonso Carmine 1894,
Giacomo 1901 — **and Filomena alone is missing from a series that holds all her siblings.**

**So: for ANGELO the absence is the source's fault and the premise stands. For FILOMENA it is not, and
the premise is what should be doubted.** An absence is only evidence when the source would otherwise
have shown the thing — **and the way to tell is to count what the source holds around it.**
