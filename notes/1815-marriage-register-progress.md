**Image 8, right page**, read at native resolution (3303 px):

> **LUIGI FALCO** di anni **diciotto compiti**, di professione **Sapunaro**, domiciliato nel Comune
> de' **SEI CASALI**, **strada Croce di San Felice**, **minore**, assistito da **SEBASTIANO FALCO**
> suo Padre della stessa professione e domiciliato, e da **COLOMBA MONTEFUSCO** sua Madre di anni
> **cinquantacinque**, di professione **filatrice**, domiciliati in casa di suo marito, presenti
> all'atto, e dando il loro consenso.
>
> E comparsa egualmente **[?]ietta RENCHIELLO(?)** di anni **diciotto**, domiciliata in questa
> suddetta Comune, **strada del Borgo**, di professione **filatrice**, **minore**, assistita da
> **[An]gela [?]** sua madre di anni **cinquantasei**, di professione contadina…

**Three things at once.**

1. **A Falco household this archive has never seen** — **SEBASTIANO FALCO × COLOMBA MONTEFUSCO**,
   with a son **Luigi** born about 1797.
2. **They are not of Arienzo. They live in the «Comune de' Sei Casali»** — which is
   **SAN FELICE A CANCELLO**, the town that calls itself the Six Hamlets of Arienzo in its own
   acts. Their street is **strada Croce di San Felice**.
3. **Their trade is *SAPUNARO* — soap-maker**, father and son. Every Falco this archive has traced
   is a *contadino*, a *colono* or a *bracciale*. This is a trade, and an unusual one.

### What is and is not claimed

**No connection to this line is claimed.** Nothing joins Sebastiano Falco to Matteo Falco, and the
archive's standing rule is that a shared surname in one district is suggestive and not evidence.

**But the coincidence is worth stating plainly, because it is the kind that misleads.**
Generation four's wife, **ANGELA ROSA MORGILLO, was born at San Felice on 24 February 1818** —
three years after this act, in the town where these Falco lived. This archive has spent years
assuming San Felice was a place the family merely *married into*. There were Falco living there.

**That is a question, not an answer**, and the trade cuts against kinship as much as the surname
cuts for it.

### What would test it

- **San Felice's own registers**, newly reachable — see `notes/antenati-holdings-discovery.md`.
  A death act for Sebastiano Falco or Colomba Montefusco would name Sebastiano's parents.
- Luigi Falco was **eighteen in 1815**, so born about **1797**; his baptism should be in San
  Felice's parish books.
- The **processetto** for this marriage: Arienzo 1815, `an_ua14156`, bundle number not yet
  identified. It would carry both baptism extracts.

## What the second half gave that the first did not

**A GENTRY CARFORA MARRIAGE, with two deaths of the 1780s.** Act 20 is not a *contadino* wedding at
all: **Signor PASQUALE CARFORA, *medico cerusico*** — a surgeon — of Santa Maria a Vico, marrying
**Signora Alessandra de Angelis, *gentildonna*, a native of NAPLES**. His parents are named as
**the late Signori PAOLO CARFORA and ANNA SABATELLI**, with their death dates recited from the acts
produced: **6 November 1782** and **22 January 1789**. Work-list row 63 exists because this archive
did not know there were Carfora at Arienzo to look for; this is the earliest dated Carfora couple it
now holds, and they are *gentry*, which is a different social register from the Carfora of the
Annecchino line. **Nothing joins them and nothing is claimed.**

**A COSSI HOUSEHOLD, which is the control this archive has needed.** Act 21 names **ELISABETTA
COSSI**, twenty-five, daughter of the late **MARINO COSSI**. The surname is written twice in one
hand and reads **C + round o + double long ſ + i** both times: there is no *i* between the C and the
o, which is what a *Cioffi* would have, and no narrow *r*, which is what a *Crisci* would have.
**COSSI is therefore a real and distinct Arienzo surname** — which matters, because this archive has
spent months correcting *Cossi* to *Cioffi* on strada Camellara and needed a documented Cossi
elsewhere to show the correction was about **one household**, not about the name.

**And one age that does not work.** Act 18 gives **Anna Martone** as «*di anni ventinove compiti*»
while making her father **forty**. A man of forty has no daughter of twenty-nine. She is also called
**minore**, which fits a teenager and not a woman of twenty-nine. The likeliest reading is
**diciannove**, but the word was not magnified and **the act is recorded here as it stands, with the
arithmetic flagged** — the same pairing of «minore» against a stated age that corrected Giovanna
d'Ambrogio in 1850, pointing the other way.

> PETRILLO**, come dall'atto di morte a noi esibito…

**Three readings corrected, and one of them was mine, caught before it was published.**

1. **The surname is PETRILLO.** At the width used for the survey this act reads **«Vecchiello»**,
   and that is what this pass was going to publish. At **twelve times** it is *P‑e‑t‑r‑i‑l‑l‑o*:
   eight letters, one dotted i, and **no second e before the double l**. The 1815 marriage index,
   read on the same day, gives Petrillo too. *A word that changes when you magnify it was never
   read.*
2. **Her forename is disputed.** The **act** says **STELLA** — an initial that does not descend
   below the line, then *t‑e‑l‑l‑a*. The **index** says **GIULIA** — a G with a full descending
   loop, then *i‑u‑l‑i‑a*. This archive had published **«Giustina»**, which is neither. Her parents
   are identical in both, so she is one woman; both forenames are recorded and neither preferred.
3. **Her mother is BRIGIDA COSSI**, fifty-six, not the «[An]gela [?]» the note above carried.

## The COSSI test, corrected

This archive's three-way surname test says *Cioffi* has **a tall double-f ligature with a long
descender** and *Cossi* **a short double-s with no ascender**. It was derived from the 1825
register. **It does not hold in this hand.** Read at twelve times, the doubled letters of COSSI here
descend exactly as a double f would — and so do those of **MASSONE** and **SANSONE**, which are
certainly a long ſ.

**What separates them in any hand is the letter count.** *C‑i‑o‑f‑f‑i* is six letters and carries an
**i between the C and the o**; *C‑o‑s‑s‑i* is five and does not. Three instances in this one volume —
**BRIGIDA COSSI** here, **ELISABETTA** and **MARINO COSSI** in act 21 — all show the C running
straight into a round o with nothing between.

**So COSSI is a documented Arienzo surname**, borne by two households in the 1815 marriages alone,
and the long correction of *Cossi* to *Cioffi* on **strada Camellara** was about **one household**
and never about the name.

## What the first half adds

**COSSI, three more times, and it settles the surname.** **BRIGIDA** (act 8), **CHIARA** (act 11)
and **FAUSTINA** (act 12), beside **ELISABETTA** and **MARINO** in act 21. **Five instances in one
volume in one hand**, every one written *C + round o + double long ſ + i*, with **no i between the C
and the o**. Cossi is a real and common Arienzo surname and the long correction of *Cossi* to
*Cioffi* on strada Camellara was about one household.

**DOROTEA CARFORA**, forty-nine, a *bracciale*'s wife of strada Rosciano — born about 1766, and a
third social register of Carfora beside the gentry surgeon of act 20 and the *contadini* of strada
Camellara in 1831. **None of the three is joined to any other.**

**A DI GUIDA WITH A DATED DEATH.** Act 1's groom is «*vedovo di **MARGARITA DI GUIDA**, morta in
questa stessa comune il giorno **quindici** del mese di **Ottobre** dell'anno **milleottocento­
quattordici***». Recorded as a lead. Nothing joins her to **Antonia di Guida**, and the coincidence
that she died on the day generation three married is exactly that.

**Two strada Camellara households**: **Domenica Melillo**, daughter of the late Mattia Melillo and
Alessandra Mauriello (act 10), and the **Verdicchio** shoemakers, father and son, of act 11.

*One caution recorded rather than resolved.* At contact-sheet resolution the 1815 marriage **index**
appeared to number Michele Monaco **13** where the register numbers him **15**. The index was not
read at magnification and a contact sheet is not a reading, so **nothing is claimed** — it is flagged
for whoever reads that index properly.
