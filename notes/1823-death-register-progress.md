## No Falco died in 1823 — but the surname is named three times

### «GIUSEPPE FALCO FU MATTEO» — twice in two days

**Image 20 (right), 20 June**, at the death of **Maria Crisci**, three, daughter of **Marcello
Crisci** and **Maria Rosa Mauro**:

> …sono comparsi **CESARE GIGLIO**, di anni **cinquanta**, *Contadino*, domiciliato in questo
> comune, **strada Camellara**, *vicino della defunta*, e **GIUSEPPE FALCO FU MATTEO**, di anni
> **quarantatre**, di professione **Contadino**, domiciliato **strada Camellara**…

**Image 21 (right), atto 39, the same day**, at the death of **Antonia Cossi**, nine months:

> …sono comparsi **GIUSEPPE FALCO FU MATTEO**, di anni **quarantadue**, *Contadino*, domiciliato in
> questo comune, **strada Sant'Andrea**, *vicino della defunta*, ed **ANIELLO GIGLIO**, di anni
> **quarantasei** … **figlia di FRANCESCO COSSI** … **E DI MARIA FALCO**.

Plates: `giuseppe-falco-fu-matteo-1823.jpg`, `maria-falco-cossi-1823.jpg`

**This is the first time the CIVIL series states Giuseppe Falco's parentage outright.** The clerk
writes *«fu Matteo»* to tell him from the town's other Giuseppe Falco, and in doing so confirms,
from a series wholly separate from the parish books, that **Giuseppe of strada Camellara is a son of
the late Matteo Falco** — brother to Pasquale, Rosa and Angela.

**Two cautions the pair of acts hands us free of charge.** He is **forty-three on one day and
forty-two on the next**, from the same clerk — the plainest possible measure of how loosely these
ages are kept. And he is written at **strada Camellara** in one and **strada Sant'Andrea** in the
other: in this form a declarant called *«vicino»* is recorded at the street of the house where the
death happened, so **a declarant's street is not by itself evidence of where he lived**. That
qualifies a good deal of what this archive has recorded from declarant lines.

### A third Maria Falco, living

The mother of the dead child in atto 39 is a **MARIA FALCO**, wife of **Francesco Cossi**, alive in
1823. She is neither the Maria Falco buried on 24 July 1817 (daughter of Giuseppe Falco and Rosa
Sossi, widow of Francesco Quotolo) nor the Maria Falco already dead in February 1818 as Angela
Majone's mother. **Her parents are not named**, and Giuseppe Falco declares as a *vicino* and not as
kin, so nothing in the act joins her to this line.

## PAOLO RIVETTI — Crescenzo's brother, and Angela Falco's brother-in-law

**Image 55 (right), 9 October:**

> …**è morto nella sua propria casa PAOLO RIVETTI, di anni cinquanta, marito di ANGELA
> ESPOSITELLA**, nato in Arienzo, *Contadino*, domiciliato ivi **strada Fontana**, **figlio di
> PASQUALE RIVETTI Defunto**, di professione contadino, **e di GIOVANNA PERROTTA**, domiciliata ivi.

Plate: `paolo-rivetti-death-1823.jpg`

Those are exactly the parents the 1816 act gives for **CRESCENZO RIVETTI**, the man who married
**Angela Falco**, daughter of Matteo and Francesca. **So Paolo is Crescenzo's brother**, born about
1773 — Angela's brother-in-law.

**Two more things fall out of it.** *Giovanna Perrotta is written without «fu»* — **she was still
alive in October 1823**, seven years after burying Crescenzo and a year after burying her
daughter-in-law Angela. And the declarant is **GIOVANNI RIVETTI, twenty-three, a *possidente* of
strada Capodiuomo**, who gives himself as ***nipote*** — nephew. **That ties the propertied Rivetti
of Capodiuomo to this labouring family**, and is a third strike against the clean gentry/labourer
split this archive drew after 1821.

## The Rivetti, and why the tidy split keeps failing

This volume alone names Rivetti at every level and on five streets:

- **Monticello de' Cappuccini** — Vincenzo Rivetti (32, son of **Paolo Rivetti** and **Francesca
  delli Paoli**), declared by **Don Giuseppe Rivetti, *sacerdote***, and Antonio Rivetti, **both
  «parente del defunto»**. The priest is kin to the contadini.
- **Capodiuomo** — **Giovanni Rivetti** and **Pasquale Rivetti**, both ***possidenti***; Raffaele
  Rivetti and Faustina Cimmino.
- **Camellara** — **Giuseppe Rivetti** and **Chiara Sorrino**, who lost two children in two days
  (Paolo, three, on 23 August; Grazia, seven months, on 24 August).
- **Cossi** — Gennaro Rivetti and Carmina Perrino.
- **Sant'Andrea** — Salvatore Rivetti and Anna Gimbardo.
- **Porta di Sopra** — **Don Giovan Battista Rivetti, *farmacista***, whose three-year-old son died
  in November; his wife Donna Caterina Espositella had died in February 1820.
- **Terra Murata** — a third living Nicola Rivetti, a *fruttajolo*, and Maria Rosa Giglio.

**A surname that runs from day-labourers to a pharmacist, a priest and two propertied men — and
whose branches call each other kin — cannot be sorted by station.** The open question about Chiara
Rivetti stands where 1821 left it: two rival candidate couples, neither established.

## Institutions and trades this volume reveals

- **A Capuchin monastery at Arienzo.** *«Padre Ludovico, nel secolo chiamato **SIMONE SGAMBATO**»*,
  eighty-two, died in the **Monastero de' Cappuccini di questo comune**. That explains *strada
  Cappuccini* and *strada Monticello de' Cappuccini*, both of which recur through these registers.
- **An ex-Augustinian friar**, Giuseppe Vene, fifty-six, born at Bonea — a man displaced by the
  suppression of the religious houses.
- **Suor Elena Anna Racca**, eighty, a ***conversa*** — a lay sister.
- **The Ospedale di Ave Grazia Plena** takes eight of the year's dead, always declared by Matteo and
  Domenico Migliore, *calzolai* of San Felice.
- Trades new to this archive: a ***maccaronaro*** (pasta-maker), a ***sanatore di cavalli***
  (horse-doctor), a ***farmacista***, a ***vinivendolo***, a ***tabacchiere***.

## Gentry on strada Camellara, again

**Don Alessandro de Rughi**, *gentiluomo*, lost a one-year-old daughter on **strada Camellara** in
December — as the de Angelis did in 1820. The family's street was never only labourers.
